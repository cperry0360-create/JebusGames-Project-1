# Level 8, The Optimization: built, soaked, and parked

**The CEO is 9,000 health.** 39% at 120 seeds, 43% at 480, both inside the
35-45% band.

**HR's armour aura is built and is flagged OPEN**, and the soak says more than
"unsettled": at 0, 2, 4 and 8 armour the level soaks at 40%, 40%, 39%, 40%. It
fires on 97 enemies a run — counted, because a flat table cannot tell "worth
nothing" from "never ran" — and changes nothing. As designed it is flavour.

**Level 8 is NOT reachable.** It is unlocked by level 7, level 7 has no row in
`levels.json`, and a row for level 8 today would be a permanently locked node on
the world map and a red build. Everything else is done: map, roster, waves,
rules, art, card, simulator, 20 unit tests and 29 checks in a rendered frame.

### Four traps, all four hit

- **The Intern's box is wider than he is tall** (854×567) because loose paper
  flies out behind him. His 58 is 58 of ROBOT, verified rather than assumed: his
  own ink fills rows 0-566, the full canvas height, and the paper only adds
  width (rows 140-490 out at x 0-300). What the wide box does affect is
  `fitInBox`, which divides by both extents — hence the honest
  `contentWidth`/`contentHeight` in the manifest.
- **His foot band is 0.88, the shallowest in the file**, which is the opposite
  of every other band and is the second half of the same trap: he is RUNNING,
  one foot down, and at 0.86 a sheet of paper joins the footprint and the shadow
  becomes 61 px of litter. At 0.88 only the foot is left.
- **The Office Drone is landscape** (4084×2888 of ink, as briefed) and hovers,
  so its shadow and its x anchor both come off the ink — the rule the Zamboni,
  the Glitch Bug, the Glider and the Rooster already get. `tests/manifest.test.ts`
  needed it added to `BODY_SHADOWED` with the reason, or a 100%-of-width shadow
  reads as the mismeasurement that test exists to catch.
- **The CEO is padded 80 px on every side** and the shaved outline is not
  recoverable, so his `anchorY` is 0.9812 rather than 1.0.
- **The scan sheet arrived cut** — 8 frames of 272×610 on a uniform grid — and is
  declared as a `sheet` in `art.json` and not re-sliced.

## 3. The map, and the two exits

`src/data/map_level8.json` is **generated** by `tools/build_level8_map.py` from
`tools/level8_geometry.json`. Nothing is hand-edited, and the map adds exactly
three coordinates to the traced road:

| lane | traced points | length | terminal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shared` (the trunk, `mainId`) | 53 + 1 gateway at x −60 | 1342.2 px | forks at (1072, 356) |
| `east` | 17 + 1 gateway at x 1340 | 436.7 px | (1279, 544) |
| `south` | 71 + 1 gateway at y 779 | 2304.3 px | (1076, 719) |

**Routes: east 1778.9 px, south 3646.5 px.** The brief's "about 4,036 world px,
roughly 67 seconds" is the geometry file's `roadLength` — the trunk plus BOTH
branches — rather than a walk: the longest thing an enemy actually walks is
3,646.5 px, which is 60.8 s at 60 px/s. `laneLengthPx` on the row will be
3646.5.

**No `entrance` and no `exit` block is written**, which is how the two exits
work with no new code. Those two are read off the plate as map X positions and
converted per lane by `Gateway.laneGates`; level 8's roads leave through three
different edges, so no single x means "the gate" on all three lanes. Omitting
both is what levels 2 to 5 do, and `laneGates` then falls back to each lane's
own full length — so every enemy walks to the end of the road it is on and leaks
there. **That fallback IS level 5's two-exit support**, and `GameScene.leak`
charges the enemy's own `livesCost` with no reference to which exit it was, so
"both exits cost lives" is a property of there being no code that asks.

Level 8 is the first map to use the SPLIT (`mainMerge` with two continuations),
which shipped with level 5's crossroads and which level 5's own map does not
use — its three gates merge into one exit.

**One gateway is not a computed heading, and that is a finding.** The south
branch's last traced stub runs flat along the bottom row of the mask, because
the mouth spreads to 153 px where it meets the frame; a heading measured across
it comes out 25° off vertical and puts the gateway at x 1203, 127 px to the side
of a mouth centred on 1076. A road that leaves through a horizontal edge leaves
DOWNWARD. Level 6's flank is the same case at the same edge.

### 19 pads, and the east arm is the cheap exit

Pads exactly as the geometry file lists them. `python3 tools/check_level8.py`
agrees with the plate on every one of them:

```
  shared spine 51.0  geometry 51  0.00%  (gate 8%)
  the bottom mouth is 153 px = 3.00 road widths (brief: about 2.8)
  19 pads, every core on carpet (one has a 2px/2-blob rounding), closest pair 75.5 px
  the plate and tools/level8_geometry.json agree.
```

Coverage, from the geometry file: **shared 71%, south 77%, east 36%**. The east
arm is the cheap exit, and section 6 measures what that is worth.

## 5. The three new mechanics

All three resolve through `Levels.levelRules`, which returns null for every
level that names no rules file — so each is one `if` from not existing anywhere
else. Section 7's before-and-after soak is the proof rather than the claim.

### The Performance Review, measured off the plate

`tools/find_level8_gate.py` finds the only saturated red blob on the painting —
506 px, 63.1 px long at 69.9° — fits its principal axis, projects it a quarter
of its length past each painted end, and intersects it with the SHIPPED lane
polylines from `map_level8.json`.

**It lands on the south branch, 142.68 px past the fork, at world (954.7,
422.8).** One crossing, not two. The brief warns the road doubles back and an
enemy can cross twice; on this plate it does not, and the tool reports every
crossing it finds rather than the first.

That placement is the most consequential thing the tool found and the brief
could not have known it: **an enemy routed east is never reviewed.** The gate is
on the long, well-covered arm, so the waves are built around it.

The engine side is a **distance test, not a proximity test**: an enemy carries
the distance it has walked down its own lane, and `crossedGate` asks whether
that number passed the gate's. A 25 px circle would be stepped clean over by a
174 px/s Intern on a 400 ms frame. The merge case is asked rather than assumed —
if the step crossed the junction, `continuations` and `pickAt` (the same two
pure functions the walker moved with) say which arm it took and at what distance
it joined.

Buffed **once, ever**: the latch is `Enemy.reviewed`, so a second crossing, a
child spawned past the line and a boss all take the same path. Bosses are immune
on `role`/`tier`. The buff is +10% rendered size and ×1.2 speed, and the speed
sits in its own `reviewSpeed` multiplier beside `speedScale` rather than
overwriting the field level 5's sky owns.

`fx_performance_scan` plays at the crossing point. `fx_performance_buff` hangs
over a reviewed enemy **for the rest of its life** through
`GameScene.syncStatusMarkers`, which asks every enemy what is true of it each
frame and therefore cannot leak a marker — the lesson `fx_mind_control` taught.
The marker is deliberately ONE FRAME and carries no `sheet`, so
`registerEffectAnims` never builds an animation for it and there is none to
outlive its texture; the scan sheet does go through that path, and
`forgetEffectAnims` drops it by asking the texture.

### HR's armour aura — built, and flagged open

`systems/EnemyAura.ts`. +4 armour inside 110 px (just under the shortest tower
range, 112), through `Enemy.effectiveArmor` — the one place armour becomes
damage taken, so every damage path respects it without knowing it exists.
Recomputed every frame from whatever HR is alive, so it ends the instant she
dies with nothing to unwind. **The largest in range, not the sum**: four HR
together are +4, not +16, which is the answer slows and the Beacon already give.

**And it does nothing to the level.** See section 6.

### The Consultant's explosion — both sides

`level8.json`'s `deathBlast`: 70 px, 30 damage, `hitsPlayerUnits: true`. It
reuses `damageEnemy` for the enemies caught and the hero's, the gnomes' and the
lads' own `hurt` for the player's side, plus the same `ART.fx.blast` every other
explosion plays. The corpse is excluded from its own blast — not only tidiness:
without it the call recurses on the frame it dies. 70 px is 1.4 road widths, so
it catches the pack behind it and cannot reach a pad at the 90 px standoff every
one of them sits at.

### The CEO

Two summon phases, 70% and 40%, four Office Drones each, **capped at six
alive**. `onHealthThreshold` now takes a list as well as one object — the shape
`LaneDef.merge` uses — and each entry is latched independently, so a boss healed
back above 70% does not re-summon and the 40% phase is not spent by the first
crossing. Batula's single-object row is untouched.

## 6. The soak

**Hero rotation: none.** `tools/soak/level.ts` passes no hero, so every run below
is CORY. `run.ts` is the driver that rotates by seed.

Level 8 has no row, so `level.ts` registers the row it will have in a `PARKED`
table, and `Sim.ts` now reports a `wrong-level` finding when a soak is pointed
at an id that resolves to something else. Without that pair a soak of level 8
silently printed **level 1's** win rate under level 8's name.

### What the simulator was taught, and one bug it was hiding

Modelled: the two exits and which one each leak came out of, the one-time gate
buff with its non-stacking rule and its merge case, HR's aura, the Consultant's
blast on both sides, and the CEO's two phases with their cap.

**`onHealthThreshold` was gated on `night`** — the whole block sat behind
`night &&` because the only threshold in the game was Batula's, whose grant is a
Humiliation stack. The CEO's phases would have fired NOWHERE in the simulator
and his health would have been tuned against a boss that never summons. That is
level 4's Beacon again, and it is exactly what the brief warned about.

Approximated, and stated: **the gnomes are not modelled at all** in that file,
so the friendly half of the Consultant's blast is measured light; the SIZE half
of the gate's buff is not modelled, because nothing there draws anything; and
the ready phase is unmodelled, so every win rate is a floor (the header of
`Sim.ts` has that in full).

### The sensitivity table, 120 seeds, one variable at a time, reverting between rows

`tools/soak/tune8.ts`, which takes the FILE as well as the path because two of
the three mechanics live in the rules block rather than on an enemy row.

    ceo.maxHealth                      5000 -> 84%   7000 -> 73%   9000 -> 39%
                                      11000 -> 13%  13000 ->  1%

    every wave count, scaled            x0.5 ->  2%   x0.75 -> 15%   x1.0 -> 39%
                                        x1.3 -> 66%

    the four rewards, x the brief's      x1.0 ->  3%   x1.3 -> 14%   x1.6 -> 32%
                                        x1.63 -> 39%  x1.75 -> 47%   x1.9 -> 60%

    level8.json armorAura.armorBonus       0 -> 40%      2 -> 40%      4 -> 39%
                                           8 -> 40%

    level8.json performanceReview
                .speedMultiplier         1.0 -> 41%    1.2 -> 39%    1.5 -> 41%

    level8.json deathBlast.damage          0 -> 38%     30 -> 39%     80 -> 43%

**The table is not flat, and the second row is the finding: taking enemies out
of this level makes it HARDER, monotonically.** 19 pads is the largest board in
the game — levels 2 to 6 carry 15, 15, 14, 14 and 18 — and the brief's
provisional rewards sat at the house median of about 0.12 peanuts per point of
enemy health, which funds about ten towers over thirteen waves and leaves half
the board empty. On this board enemies are net INCOME, so the wave table is the
purse. At the briefed rewards the level soaks at **3%** and the simulated player
does not reach the CEO with lives in hand.

So the rewards are **1.63x the brief's** — 13, 33, 44, 62 — which is 0.125 to
0.2 peanuts per point of health: between levels 1-4 (0.08-0.13) and level 6
(0.27-0.44) rather than outside the game. Every other lever was tried first and
is in the table.

**The three new mechanics are flavour at their current numbers**, and that is
measured rather than assumed:

- **The aura fires and is worth nothing.** "Worth nothing" and "never ran" give
  the same flat table, so the simulator was instrumented to tell them apart:
  **96.6 enemies a run** stand inside an HR's aura, out of about 267 spawned.
  Four flat points of armour against a pool whose shots are 27 and 44 is a
  rounding error over a whole run.
- **The gate is worth nothing measurable**, at 6.4 enemies buffed a run. It only
  crosses the south arm, which is the 77%-covered one.
- **The explosion makes the level EASIER as it gets stronger** — +5 points of win
  rate at 80 damage — because it kills its own side faster than it hurts the
  player's. It does hurt them: 150 damage a run lands on the hero and the lads.
  The joke works mechanically.
