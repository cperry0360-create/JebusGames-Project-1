# Level 6, built

Two lanes, eighteen pads, a row on the world map and a Rooster worth 7,500.
And two things found by looking that nobody asked about: the flame was never
wired into the game at all, and it crosses lanes.

## The headline

- **The Rooster is 7,500 health.** 203/480 (42%) on normal, inside the 35-45%
  band, 80% of its losses on the boss wave.
- **The `_conflict` was that the plate merges the two lanes.** Cory chose to
  fabricate the missing road rather than re-paint. It cost **one 82 px segment**,
  39 px of which is over unpainted ground.
- **Level 6 is reachable**: slot 6 on the road, locked at four levels cleared,
  open at five, verified on a rendered world map.

## 1. The `_conflict`, and how it was resolved

`tools/level6_geometry.json` opens with:

> THE PLATE MERGES THE TWO LANES. west12 and west21 join at the junction below
> and share the last stretch to east73; east83 is fed only by a band entering at
> the bottom frame edge and is reachable from no entrance. The brief for this
> level says the lanes are independent and that each exit has its own entrance.
> They are not, and it does not.

Confirmed three ways before anything was built on it:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `lanes.north` and `lanes.south` last points | both `[1279, 526]` — the same exit |
| trailing points they share | **15**, from `[1070, 466]` |
| orphan band's minimum x | **92** — it never touches the west edge |
| `check_level6.py`, both entrances → east83 | **NO PATH IN THE PAINT** |

So the design ("two independent lanes, each with its own entrance and exit")
and the art are irreconcilable, and no reading of the geometry file makes them
agree. Three ways out existed: re-paint the plate, re-design level 6 as a merge
map, or author the missing road. **Cory chose to author it**, over a
recommendation to accept the paint. That is recorded here because it is a
decision, not a derivation.

### What was fabricated, exactly

**One segment on the south lane: `(591, 393) → (592, 475)`, 82.0 px.**

Both endpoints are traced points — `(591,393)` from `lanes.south`, `(592,475)`
from `orphanBand.centreline`. What is invented is the line between them.

| property | value |
|---|---|
| fabricated length | 82.0 px |
| of which over ground with **no road painted** | **39.0 px** |
| props crossed | none |
| pads crossed | none |
| solid % swept across the full 40 px road width | 13.1% |
| …against the **painted lanes' own** baseline | 12.1-14.1% |

That last row is the control and it is the reason the "solid" number means
nothing on its own: sweeping a 40 px band along the *painted* north and south
lanes reads 13.9% and 14.1% solid, because the artist drew a dark kerb along
each road. The connector reads **at** that baseline, so it crosses no prop. What
it does cross is bare floor, and `floor%` is the honest discriminator: the
painted lanes read **0.0%** floor, the connector 37.5%.

It was chosen as the **narrowest** gap between the south lane and the
unreachable band. Every alternative was worse on fabrication:

| join | route | fabricated | pads reaching no road | lower-lane coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **shipped** — `upper[23]` | 1483 | **82.0** | 5 | 48.7% |
| `upper[2]` from `south[5]` | 1593 | 200.7 | 4 | 54.0% |
| `upper[0]` from `south[5]` | 1755 | 278.0 | 4 | 54.4% |
| round the U-turn, `lower[16]` | 2591 | 161.5 | **2** | 46.2% |

The shipped join minimises the thing a player will actually see. The cost is
that it strands the most pads and gives the lower lane the least cover — and
both of those are recorded below rather than hidden.

**It will show.** An enemy walks about 39 px over bare ground at roughly
`(591, 434)`. Nothing in code removes that; re-painting the plate so the south
road runs through is what removes it.

## 2. The map

`src/data/map_level6.json`, generated by `tools/build_level6_map.py` — re-run it
and the diff should be empty.

| | |
|---|---|
| `mainId` | `upper` |
| upper (the map's own `waypoints`) | 81 points, west 11.8% → east 73.1%, route **1711.1** |
| lower | 61 points, west 20.8% → east 82.8%, route **1605.7** |
| `roadWidth` / `spotRadius` | 40 / 34 |
| `buildSpots` | 18, copied from the geometry file untouched |
| closest the two lanes come | **59.6 px** centre-to-centre = 19.6 px of bare floor |

`laneLengthPx` in `levels.json` is **1711.1**, the longer of the two routes.

### The gateway points are measured a road width back

The only other non-traced coordinates are the four gateway points that run each
lane off the plate. They are computed on the heading the road already has,
sampled **40 px (one road width) back** — not off the closing segment. The north
lane's last traced step is `(1277,524) → (1279,526)`: a 2 px stub at 45°, and a
gateway placed on *that* line leaves the plate at **y=587** instead of **y=534**,
a road diving off the map. Level 4's `_waypoints` note records the same trap in
the same words. It was hit here and caught before it shipped.

### Two engine changes, both forced by this shape

1. **`validateLanes` rejected two independent lanes.** It reported the second
   terminal as *"a route with no gate"* — a rule written to catch a forgotten
   `merge` back when every multi-lane map was a fork feeding one gate. A lane may
   now declare **`entrance: true`**, and `laneDefs` marks the map's own
   `waypoints` lane as one implicitly.

   This corrects `reports/2026-09-10-level-6-geometry.md` §7, which stated
   *"`validateLanes()` does not require lanes to connect to each other, so two
   disjoint lanes raise nothing."* Run against a two-independent-lane map it
   returns exactly one problem. Everything else that report credited **is**
   correct and was re-checked: `transferFrom` returns null per lane,
   `terminals(id)` and `routeLengths(id)` answer per starting lane,
   `Enemy.leaked` reads the gate of the lane being walked.

2. **`MapDef.mainId`.** `waves.level6.json` spawns on `upper` (20 groups) and
   `lower` (18). One of the two has to be the map's own `waypoints`, and that
   lane was always called `main` — so every `upper` spawn would have resolved
   through `LaneNetwork.lane()`'s unknown-id fallback: the right road, for the
   wrong reason, until a third lane arrived. The map names its main lane now.

## 3. Two deliberate properties, and a third nobody knew about

### (a) The pad standoff — the brief's numbers are superseded

The brief says 48-84 world px, median 57, against levels 2-4's 90-114. Those are
not the same measurement: 48-84 is centre-to-**paint-edge** and 90-114 is
centre-to-**centreline**. Measured like for like (and the 2026-09-10 report
established this):

| level | centre → centreline | one 112-range tower covers |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | **70.0 - 103.1, median 81.1** | **154.5 px of lane** |
| 8 | 90.1 - 101.0 | 130.0 px |
| 4 | 90.9 - 111.0 | 93.0 px |
| 3 | 91.7 - 112.0 | 90.7 px |

**20% closer, not half** — but 1.7x the coverage per gun, because what a tower
buys is the chord and the chord grows faster than the standoff shrinks. The
brief's *conclusion* stands unchanged and is the one that matters: the Rooster is
soaked against this board and is comparable to no other level's boss. No pad was
moved.

### (b) A 40 px road, and what it does to the Bruiser

The road is **40** world px against a house standard of 50 (the brief says 43;
the plate says 40). The Bruiser is the widest regular enemy at `displayHeight`
85 → 96.1 px wide, with a 65.1 px ground shadow.

**Its shadow alone is 63% wider than the road**, overhanging about 12.5 px each
side. On level 5's 62 px road the same shadow overhangs by 1.5 px.

And `Enemy.ts` computes `room = max(0, laneHalfWidth − displayWidth/2) × 0.72`.
With `laneHalfWidth` 20 and the *smallest* enemy in this roster 60.3 px wide,
`room` is **0 for all four** — every enemy walks the exact centreline, so splash
covers a line here and not a band. The art was **not** rescaled.

### (c) Five pads reach no road — four of them because of the join

`check_level6.py` has always named pad 2 at `(1167, 53)` as dead: 248.8 px from
any painted lane against a shortest tower range of 112.

On the shipped map there are **five**: pads **2, 9, 12, 16 and 18**. The extra
four were painted to serve the orphan band's *lower* arm and the left end of its
upper arm, and the shipped join uses neither. Coverage is uneven as a result:

| lane | on-plate road within 112 of some pad |
|---|---|
| upper | 66.6% |
| lower | **48.6%** |

This is the direct cost of choosing the shortest join (§1) and is the strongest
argument for re-painting. `tests/level6map.test.ts` pins the set, so moving the
join changes the test and the change is visible.

## 4. The flame was never in the game

**`src/systems/Flame.ts` was imported by `tools/soak/Sim.ts` and by
`tests/level6.test.ts` and by nothing else.** `GameScene` had no reference to it
and no reference to `def.flame`. The Rooster — `damage: 0`, `blockable: false` —
walked the level and did nothing at all.

Which makes the soak's number a statement about a game that did not exist. The
brief warned about exactly this class of error in the other direction ("a
simulator that ignores the flame produces a confident wrong number"); here the
simulator was right and the scene was empty.

### It is worth twelve points, in the direction nobody expects

| | 480 seeds, normal |
|---|---|
| with the flame (the sim, and now the scene) | **203/480 (42%)** |
| with `level6.json`'s `flame` block removed | **142/480 (30%)** |

**The flame helps the player.** Its damage only ever touches the player's own
side — hero, summoned fighters, the Ima Dummy's lads — and none of that decides
a run. What decides it is `held`: the Rooster stands still for one second in
six, and a second of a boss not walking is a second of every gun on the board
still having it in range.

So without it level 6 plays at 30% and is *out of band on the hard side*.

### Wired through the same module the soak runs

`GameScene.tickRooster` calls `tickFlame`/`inFlame`/`flameEnd` directly, so the
win rate the soak reports and the fight the player has are the same arithmetic.
The corridor and the scorch marker are **static images** — no animation is
registered, deliberately, so the `forgetEffectAnims` trap from `9e389b8` cannot
re-open here. If the flame ever gets frames it goes through that path.

**It went in the wrong place first.** `tickRooster` was called from inside
`tickNight`, which returns on its first line when a level has no `phases` block —
and level 6 has none, that is level *five's* rules block. It therefore did
nothing whatsoever. `sh tools/harness/run.sh level6` is what caught it: the
Rooster spawned, walked, and never once stopped or breathed.

### And it crosses lanes, which the data claimed was impossible

`level6.json`'s `_flame` note said:

> And it cannot leave its lane — the corridor runs along the heading the boss is
> walking and level 6's two lanes are parallel and never meet, so that is a
> property of the map rather than a check in code.

It is not true. The corridor is a **straight** 300 px capsule and both roads
serpentine:

| breathing from | reaches the other lane at |
|---|---|
| the upper lane | **25.7%** of its length |
| the lower lane | **7.3%** of its length |

Measured twice and independently — once inside the running game by the harness
scenario, once offline against the shipped map file — agreeing to 0.3 points
(the harness samples every 4 px, the offline pass every 2 px).

Nothing was changed to hide it: `reach` and `width` are both in `level6.json`
and either one shortens the corridor. The note now states the numbers instead of
the claim. **This is a decision waiting for Cory**, not a bug with an obvious fix.

### The scorch is real but position-dependent

The corridor is 64 px wide (half-width 32) and every pad on this board sits
70-103 px off its lane. So whether a breath scorches anything depends entirely on
*where* it is taken: **21.4%** of the upper lane and **20.1%** of the lower can
reach a pad from some position; the rest cannot reach one from any. Eight of the
eighteen pads are reachable from the upper lane, five from the lower.

The harness check was luck-based at first and reported "no scorch" for a
mechanic that works. It now parks the boss on a position whose corridor covers
the tower and holds him there between breaths — he walks 180 px in a six-second
cadence, which was enough to drift off the spot every time.

## 5. The soak

Full tables are in `SOAK-REPORT.md` under **2026-09-11**. The short version:

**Hero: Cory, and only Cory.** `tools/soak/level.ts` calls
`simulate(seed, 'normal', LEVEL, undefined, DIFFICULTY)` — the fourth argument is
the hero and it passes `undefined`, so `Sim.ts` resolves `DEFAULT_HERO_ID` for
all 480 seeds. The by-seed rotation belongs to `run.ts`, the whole-game soak, and
none of these numbers used it. No hero's values were touched.

### Does `Sim.ts` model this level?

- **Two independent lanes: yes.** It builds the full `LaneNetwork`, spawns carry
  `laneId`, and `padToLane`/`padToExit` both walk `net.lanes` rather than main.
- **The flame: exactly**, sharing `systems/Flame.ts` with the scene — the
  six-second cadence, the 0.8 s telegraph, the rule that a boss killed
  mid-telegraph burns nothing, the corridor's geometry including its round tip,
  the damage per tick, the scorch's rate-halving and its four seconds.
- **Approximated: the heading.** The scene reads the boss's lane; the sim casts
  along the direction it moved last frame. Same answer on a straight run, a few
  degrees out through a bend.
- **Not modelled: the summoned fighters.** The soak never puts them on the board,
  so the fire has two kinds of victim there and three in the game.

### HP alone could not reach the band

| rooster.maxHealth | wins/120 | lost after wave |
|---|---|---|
| 1,200 | 17 (14%) | w4x24 w5x36 w6x26 w7x13 w8x3 w9x1 |
| 2,600 | 16 (13%) | w4x24 w5x36 w6x26 w7x13 w8x3 w9x1 w12x1 |
| 4,500 | 8 (7%) | …identical… w12x9 |
| 7,000 | 0 (0%) | …identical… w12x17 |
| 10,000 | 0 (0%) | …identical… w12x17 |

**The pre-boss buckets are identical in every row.** 103 of 120 runs died before
wave 13 whatever the boss cost, capping the level at 14% with a free boss. That
is the flat table the brief says to look for.

**The cause.** `waves.level6.json`'s `_curve` note reasons about *"roughly 50
seconds of walk … against 15 to 25 on every level before it"* — true of the
abandoned 2970x316 stitched plate. The shipped plate walks 1711 px: **18.6 s** at
scrapper speed, squarely in the family (levels 1-5 walk 15.6-21.5 s). The counts
were sized for a board 1.7x longer.

## 7. `plannedLevels` — the finding, nothing changed

`plannedLevels` is **20**, the campaign is **6** levels, so the world map draws
**14 padlocks** in front of Cory's kids (it was 15 before this pass).

**Changing it to 10 is a one-line JSON edit plus one test line. `WorldRoad.ts`
needs no maths redone.**

- `ROAD_SLOTS = max(LEVELS.length, plannedLevels)` in `Levels.ts` is the only
  reader.
- `nodeCentre(i)` is **purely a function of the index** —
  `x = margin + nodeW/2 + i·pitch`, `y = bandCentre() + amplitude·sin(i·step)`.
  It does not reference the total at all, so no node moves.
- `roadWidth()` uses `ROAD_SLOTS` linearly and would simply return a shorter
  road, which is the intended effect.
- Tests: **one** would fail — `tests/worldmap.test.ts:27`,
  `assert.ok(ROAD_SLOTS >= 20, 'the planned campaign is no longer on the map')`.
  Its threshold is the thing being changed, so it needs the new number.
  Everything else (`worldmap.test.ts:53,151,153`, `levels.test.ts:322,323`)
  derives from `ROAD_SLOTS` and needs nothing.

Not changed, as instructed.

## 9. What was NOT done

- **The flame's cross-lane reach was not fixed.** It is a decision (shorten
  `reach`, narrow `width`, or accept it), both numbers are data, and the note now
  states the measurement.
- **The plate was not re-painted**, so the 39 px of unpainted walking and the
  five dead pads stand.
- **`this.lane` as a stand-in for "the road"** is still wrong at the four sites
  `reports/2026-09-10-level-6-geometry.md` §7 names — `drawCoveredLane`,
  `washLane`, `validCastPoint`, `powerHazard`. `validCastPoint` is the
  functional one: `pathOnlyWithin` will refuse summons anywhere near the lower
  lane. `tickRooster` uses the boss's own lane and does not add to the list, but
  the four were not fixed.
- **The nine non-asserting harness scenarios** (`ui`, `muzzle`, `buildall`,
  `rockets`, `retreat`, `regressions`, `poor`, `typegame`, `meteor`) are
  untouched, and `GL=1` screenshots are still black for want of
  `preserveDrawingBuffer`.
- **No full playthrough of level 6 by a human or by the harness.** The scenario
  drives waves and the boss directly; it does not play thirteen waves through.
- **Difficulty modes other than normal** were not soaked for level 6.
