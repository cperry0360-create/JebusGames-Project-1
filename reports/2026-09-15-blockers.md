**Answers first.**

1. **The camera cause was the HUD pad-overlap fix, `e361efe`** — it handed
   `CameraRig` the HUD's band height as a *vertical bounds margin*, and
   `centerRange` applies a margin to the camera **centre**, which moves the wall
   outward rather than creating slack inside the plate. 137 world px of void at
   min zoom, measured.
2. **The level 8 diagnosis in the brief was correct**, in every particular,
   including the coordinates and the reason. The fix is in the generator.
3. **Vlaude CAN be killed by a maxed board, and 26,000 stands.** He dies in 195
   of the 453 soak runs that reach him. The brief's premise — that the soak
   measures an easier fight because it cannot express three of the six powers —
   **does not hold for a full board**, and all three documents that stated it
   have been corrected.

# 1 — Black screen when scrolling, on every level

## The cause, and it was the prime suspect

`reports/2026-09-13-hud-cleanup-and-level-8.md` calls its own camera change a
root-cause fix, and it was — of the pad problem. It introduced this one.

`e361efe` added two things to `CameraRig`'s limits: `boundsMarginPx` (0 in
`display.json`, so harmless on its own) and `hudBandPx`, the taller HUD band
plus a build pad's half-height, refreshed on every resize. `update` then did:

```ts
const m = this.limits.boundsMarginPx ?? 0
const my = Math.max(m, this.hudBand / Math.max(z, 0.0001))
const rx = centerRange(cam.width, this.limits.worldWidth, z, m)
const ry = centerRange(cam.height, this.limits.worldHeight, z, my)
```

`centerRange`'s `marginPx` widens the range the camera **centre** may occupy. It
does not find slack inside the plate; there is none to find. It lets the visible
rectangle leave the artwork by that much, and everything past the plate is
`display.json`'s `backgroundColor`, `#10161d` — which is the black.

`boundsMarginPx` being 0 is why the report is "scrolling up or down": the
horizontal axis got no margin and never leaked.

**From a rendered frame, before the fix** — `sh tools/harness/run.sh edges 140 844x390`:

```
at min zoom 2.33 (85.0% of the map width):
   drag left   view x 0..1088 y 138..641   inside the map
   drag right  view x 192..1280 y 138..641   inside the map
   drag up     view x 192..1280 y -136..367   *** 136px OF GREEN ***
   drag down   view x 192..1280 y 354..857   *** 137px OF GREEN ***
at max zoom 7.11 (27.8% of the map width):
   drag up     view x 738..1094 y -44..121   *** 44px OF GREEN ***
   drag down   view x 738..1094 y 600..765   *** 45px OF GREEN ***
worst overscroll anywhere: 137.0px at min/down
RESULT *** GREEN BLEEDS IN ***
```

137 and 45 are `hudBand / zoom` exactly: the band is (82 + 24) CSS px × dpr 3 =
318 device px, and 318/2.33 = 136.5, 318/7.11 = 44.7.

## Why the existing camera test was green throughout

`tests/camera.test.ts` has a test called *"the camera centre is clamped so the
view stays on the map"*, and it passed every day this bug shipped. It calls
`centerRange(vw, W, z)` — **without the margin the rig passes in**. The
guarantee it asserts was true of the function and false of the caller.

## The margin was bought for nothing

The measurement that justified it is `tests/hudpads.test.ts`, and it was
measuring something much harder than the game in two ways:

- it widened **every HUD element to the full screen width** —
  `overlaps({ ...h, x: 0, width: W }, ...)`. The HUD is a 48px readout stack in
  one corner, a 132px wave control, a 40px gear, a 300px centred message row, a
  hero chip, a 322px centred ability row and CANCEL. There is more clear width
  than covered width, so a pad slides out from under one **sideways** as
  readily as vertically, and that measurement could not see it at all.
- it required the pad's **whole bounding square** on screen, where
  `BuildSystem.spotAt` takes the nearest spot within `spotRadius` **world
  units** of the tap — a disc, and an off-centre tap on it builds.

Re-measured with the real rectangles, the real disc, and the camera clamped
strictly to the plate:

| criterion | trapped pads, 151 pads across ten levels, six viewports ± notch |
|---|---|
| the old one (HUD full-width, whole square on screen) | 47 at 667x375, 7–8 at any zoom |
| real 2-D rectangles, whole square on screen | 1 to 3, viewport-dependent |
| **real disc, a 44pt clear tap circle inside it** | **0, everywhere** |

Every pad on every level has a zoom inside the band and a camera position
inside the plate where a 44pt clear circle lands on it. It is not true **at
rest** and cannot be — at cover zoom a pad is about 30 CSS px across before any
HUD is involved, which is under the 44pt floor on its own — so the honest claim
is reachability, and the player zooms in to build. That is what `defaultZoom`
1.72 is for.

## The fix

- `centerRange` **loses its margin parameter outright**, so no caller can widen
  the clamp again. `viewSpan` is added for checks written against what the
  player sees rather than against the centre.
- `CameraRig` loses `hudBandPx`, `setHudBand` and `boundsMarginPx`.
- `boundsMarginPx` is retired from `display.json` with a note recording why.
- `hudBandHeight` stays, for measuring; its header records that it no longer
  feeds the camera.

## The test the brief asked for

`tests/camerabounds.test.ts`, three tests:

1. **No zoom or scroll the player can reach puts the view off the plate.** Ten
   viewports × four device ratios × 17 zooms across the band × both ends of the
   legal centre range and a fling past each, asserting the **visible span** is
   inside the plate to within one world pixel.
2. **The rounded scroll the rig actually writes is on the plate too** —
   `safeScroll` rounds, and the float clamp and the integer that reaches Phaser
   are not the same number.
3. **Nothing can widen the clamp again** — no `marginPx` in `CameraMath`, no
   `hudBand` or `boundsMarginPx` in the rig, exactly two `centerRange` calls
   each with three arguments, no `hudBandPx:` in `GameScene`, and
   `display.camera.boundsMarginPx === undefined`.

**Both behaviour tests were checked against a reintroduced 100px margin before
being trusted**, and both go red: `the view spans x -100.0..304.8 of a 1280
plate`.

## And the same from rendered frames, on all ten levels

`run.sh edges` used to drag four ways on one level. It now also sweeps all ten,
at both ends of the zoom band, at all four extremes — and counts **uncovered
pixels** as well as reading `worldView`, by giving the world camera a magenta
background so that any pixel the plate did not cover is unambiguous.

```
sweeping all 10 levels at the two ends of the zoom band:
   level1   inside the plate at every corner   corners: 0.00% uncovered
   ...
   level10  inside the plate at every corner   corners: 0.00% uncovered
worst overscroll anywhere: 0.0px
RESULT the view never leaves the artwork, on any of 10 levels
```

**The pixel probe took two tries and the first one was wrong**, which is worth
recording because it looked exactly like a product bug. Counting *dark* pixels
reported 43.4% void on level 9 with the camera provably inside the plate: the
clear colour is luma 21 and levels 8, 9 and 10 are painted machine worlds with
large areas darker than that. Switching to the camera background and matching
it loosely (`r>200, g<60, b>200`) then reported 0.62% on level 9 — **that board
is painted in neon magenta and the probe was counting the artwork.** An exact
match on 0xff00ff reads 0.00% on all ten. Both instruments were then checked
against a reintroduced 100px margin: 100px past the plate, 65.37% uncovered.

# 2 — Level 8 enemies backtrack from the east entrance

## The diagnosis was correct

Confirmed coordinate by coordinate, and then quantified. The east lane is the
east **exit**'s traced polyline walked the other way, so it ended at the fork,
and it merged into `south` at index 0 because that is where the exit used to
begin. `south`'s first move from the fork is back down through (1029, 399) — 3
px from a point the east arm had passed two steps earlier, after climbing 76 px
to get there.

Measured on the route rather than on the lane:

| route | worst turn | closest return to a stretch already walked |
|---|---|---|
| every other route on every level | ≤ 90.0° (two computed stubs at a frame edge) | ≥ 0.79 road widths (level 5's north arm) |
| **level 8 `east>south`, shipped** | **135.0° at (1072, 356)** | **3.2 px = 0.06 road widths** |

An outlier by a factor of twelve, and 177° when an enemy actually walks it.

## Why every check in the repository was clean

Three separate reasons, and the third is the one worth keeping:

1. `validateLanes` checks that a merge names a real lane and a real index. Both
   were real.
2. `tools/check_level8.py` and `tests/level8.test.ts` measure the **lanes**. The
   fault was at the handover between two of them and each lane was fine.
3. **A turn angle measured on the naive concatenation reads 0° at the join.**
   `atIndex: 0` makes the join coordinate appear twice — the arm's last waypoint
   and the target's first are the same point — so the segment across the join
   has zero length and no direction at all. Dropping duplicate points before
   measuring is the difference between seeing the hairpin and not.

## The fix, derived in the generator

`tools/build_level8_map.py` gains `join_to`, which truncates an arm at the last
traced point from which it can continue onto its target without turning back,
and lands the merge on the target's own nearest waypoint. It searched every
(truncation, index) pair and **rejected 532 that walked backward**. The answer
for the east arm: drop the last three traced points, end at (1028, 432), merge
at `south` index 2.

No coordinate was hand-edited. `tests/level8.test.ts` asserts the shipped
waypoints are a **prefix** of the reversed traced branch.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| east lane points | 18 | 15 |
| east route length | 2,741.0 px | **2,558.3 px** |
| worst turn on `east>south` | 135.0° | **90.0°** (the bottom-edge gateway stub, same as `west`) |
| closest return | 3.2 px (0.06 roads) | **47.5 px (0.93 roads)** |
| merge `atIndex` | 0 (the fork) | **2** |
| handover gap | 0 px (shared coordinate) | **25.0 px** |
| joins `south` at | 0 px along | 88.5 px along, upstream of the beam at 142.68 |

All 182.7 px of the length difference was the reversal.

**The 25 px handover is real and is stated rather than hidden.** The handover
sets `laneDistance`, so the enemy's position moves 25 px in one frame. It is the
smallest gap any non-reversing join of these two separately traced centrelines
can have, it is under half a 51 px road so both ends are on painted road, and
`tests/laneroutes.test.ts` pins it at half a road width so a re-trace that
widens it has to come back. A computed junction point projected onto `south`
would cut it to about 5 px; that is one more non-traced coordinate and a shift
to every `atIndex` downstream, and it was **not** taken — noted here as the
option if the hop ever reads badly in play.

## Gate coverage, before and after

`node --experimental-strip-types tools/soak/level.ts 480 level8`, same seeds:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| performance reviews a run | 81.9 | 81.2 |
| eligible enemies reaching the beam | 84.3 | 83.3 |

# 3 — Vlaude

## (a) Courtland's power, and which condition rejected it

**The only Courtland ability Vlaude refuses is Mind Control, and the condition
is `blockable: false` in `enemies.json` — not `glides`.**

From rendered frames. `run.sh vlaude` now places every tower type at max tier
and its first specialization on pad 6 — 90 px off the road, the closest of the
twelve, with him parked at route distance 300, inside the range of every gun in

actually on the board**.

**Mind Control must stay refused.** `tickControlled` deals
`health + effectiveArmor + 1` to its target when the control expires — it
executes it. On a 26,000 hp boss that is a 10-second-cooldown instant kill.
`blockable: false` is 16 of the game's 20 bosses.

**Nothing else reads hover state from the wrong place.** `this.gliding` has
exactly one reader in the game, `tickHazards`, and it is the one yesterday's fix
changed. He carries no `layer`, so `canHit` never excludes him and every tower
can shoot him. The one consequence of yesterday's fix is the Spike Strip row
above: before it, `night?.gliding` was null on level 10 and the strip *did* hit
him, contrary to the level's own data.

### THE FIRST VERSION OF THAT BLOCK REPORTED EIGHT PRODUCT BUGS AND HAD NONE

It fired `G.firePower(slot, x, y)` while holding another hero's ability def.
`firePower` does not take a def — its first line is
`abilityInSlot(this.hero.heroId, slot)` — so every non-Cory row fired **Cory's**
slot of the same number, and rows for slots Cory does not have fired nothing.
Seismic, Fireball, Star Rain, Ice Beam, Bark and Zoomies all read
`*** NOTHING ***`, which is *exactly the report being investigated*. The two
that worked were the two held beams, because `beginHeldAbility` is the one entry
point that does take a def.

Two further corrections were needed before the table above could be trusted:

- The hero must stand on him so no range check can be what refuses — and
  something near him puts 16 points on him over the window, so three refused
  abilities read `dealt 16` and looked like abilities that half worked. A
  **control** is measured per hero with no cast at all and subtracted.
- The hero walks. The first control set his position once and slept; the
  diagnostic caught him **457 px away** at the end of a window that began with
  him on the target. He is now pinned frame by frame for both windows, and a
  second control pins him 700 px away so the difference between near and far
  names the hero's own contribution.

## (b) A maxed board against 26,000 at armour 10

`tools/soak/Sim.ts` gains a read-only `FinaleBoard`: the board measured at the
top of the final wave against that wave's heaviest enemy, and a ledger of what
he actually took. It changes no number — level 10 still reads 195/480 and the
published health sensitivity table reproduces to the integer.

Two DPS quantities, because they answer different questions:

- **`boardDps`** — every shooting tower's damage per second against armour 10,
  summed, ignoring range. The same quantity
  `reports/2026-09-07-balance-verification-and-level-2.md` published as "board
  DPS vs armour 4". It is a ceiling no enemy ever takes.
- **`walkDamage`** — for each tower, its DPS × the seconds the route spends
  inside its range. On a 3,440 px road at 20 px/s the two differ by a factor of
  several, and this is the one a boss health figure has to be compared against.
- **`dealt`** — what he actually took, through the same `hurtEnemy` every damage
  path in the sim uses. Includes the slows, the hero, the abilities and the
  escorts stealing the targeting, none of which the arithmetic can see.

**480 seeds, normal, Cory** — the published method. 12 of 12 pads built on every
seed, `freePads: 0`:

| | n | guns | board DPS vs armour 10 | walkDamage | **dealt** | lowest hp fraction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| winners | 195 | 12 | **median 723** (p10 557, p90 916) | 25,125 | **26,029** | 0.000 |
| losers | 258 | 12 | **median 546** (p10 375, p90 667) | 14,708 | **19,710** | 0.242 |
| all | 453 | 12 | median 603 | 17,860 | **24,265** | 0.067 |

**He walks 172 seconds** (3,440 px at 20 px/s) and **dies in 195 of the 453 runs
that reach him — 43%.**

The clearest single number: **the median board delivers 24,265 into a 26,000 hp
boss — 93% of his health — and the median loser watches him leave at 24%.** That
is why he reads as unkillable: most boards very nearly do it.

### The theoretical maximum, which is what "fully maxed" means

Every pad at top tier with the best specialization, chosen by search over all
794 gun/support arrangements of 12 pads:

| board | DPS vs armour 10 | delivered over the 172s walk | peanuts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 × Total Loss Write-off | 1,120 | 36,904 | 11,520 |
| **8 guns under 4 Loophole Shelters** | **1,272** | **42,996** | **11,264** |

A fully maxed board kills him with 16,996 to spare. The ceiling is not the
problem; reaching the ceiling is. The soak's median winner brings 723 DPS
against that 1,272, because the draft is random and the economy is finite.

The 120-seed pass reads **33%** at 26,000 and would have recommended 24,000.

### And the caveat about three powers is withdrawn

`SOAK-REPORT.md`, `enemies.json`'s `_health` note and `CLAUDE.md` all said the
soak "provably cannot express buildLock, generateWall or generateWeapon" and
therefore "26,000 is tuned against a board that is EASIER than the one the
player gets". The first half is true. The conclusion is not, and the rules were
read rather than reasoned about:

| power | what it costs a board with every pad built on | where |
|---|---|---|
| `buildLock` | **nothing.** `lockedPadStillFires()` returns true, always. It locks pads, and a full board has nothing left to build | `systems/Vlaude.ts` |
| `generateWeapon` | **nothing — the cast is skipped.** `weaponPad` needs a pad that is neither occupied nor locked and returns null when there is none | `systems/Vlaude.ts`, `GameScene.castWeapon` |
| `generateWall` | **no tower fire at all.** `tickVlaudeWalls` has exactly ONE damage source and it is the hero. A wall denies ground to the hero and to a garrison's rally, which is real and is not board DPS | `GameScene.tickVlaudeWalls` |

And the soak's own median board **is** full at the final wave: 12 of 12 pads on
every one of the 453 seeds that reached it.

`tests/level10.test.ts` asserts all three, including the damage-source **count**
on the wall, so a change that lets a tower shoot one fails and brings somebody
back to this paragraph.

**What the soak does understate** is the fight for an **incomplete** board: a
countermeasure takes a free pad from wave 11 and holds it until one hero chews
through 1,400 hp at armour 8, and it puts a tower out for 8 seconds at a time
while it stands. That narrower claim replaces the withdrawn one in all three
documents.

### What was NOT checked

- **The three unexpressed powers are still unexpressed in the simulator.** The
  argument above is that they are inert against a full board, read off the
  rules and asserted in tests — not that the simulator now models them.
- **No measurement of live play.** Everything here is the simulator and the
  harness. Whether a player's board is full by wave 11 is a question about
  players, and the soak's answer is its own economy model.
- **The hero's and the abilities' share of the 24,265** is not broken out. The
  ledger is a total.
- **Slows are not in `walkDamage`**, so that column is a floor. `dealt` has them.
- **The control's 16 is not identified.** It reads exactly 16 for all five
  heroes, whose powered damage is 36, 34, 48, 38 and 24 against an armour that
  falls from ~9.7 to 4–7 during the window — so it is not a number of swings of
  anything. The FAR control (hero pinned 700 px away) reads **0** for every
  hero, so it needs the hero in range and nothing else on the board is touching
  him; and it is **not his swing**, because pinning his position every frame
  keeps `Hero.update` in its walking branch (`dist > 0.5`) where no swing
  fires. Range-gated, small, constant and unnamed. The conclusion does not rest
  on naming it — both windows are measured under identical conditions, so the
  subtraction is exact and the three refusals read exactly 0 after it.
