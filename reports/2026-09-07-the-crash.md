which is the screen the crash was reported on. The memory hypothesis is
supported. Its stated mechanism is not.**

## The short version

The hypothesis was that plates accumulate across scene transitions, so a player
who reaches level select after several levels is holding well over 100 MB of
plates. The retention half is true and worse than stated. The accumulation half
is wrong, and being wrong about it matters:

**Nothing accumulates, because everything was already there.** `BootScene`
queued all 146 manifest entries — including all five map plates — before the
title screen drew a frame. The 134.5 MB of plates was resident from the loading
bar, not built up over a session. Nothing anywhere in `src/` called
`TextureManager.remove`, so nothing was ever given back either.

That distinction is the whole reason the sighting "once at the title screen on a
fresh load" is not an anomaly to be explained away. Under the accumulation
story, a fresh title screen is the *cheapest* moment in a session and should
never crash. Under what is actually happening, it is already carrying 304.3 MB.

## Step 1 — the four measurements

Taken with a new harness scenario, `tools/harness/run.sh texmem`, which walks
the real scenes and reads Phaser's texture manager at each point. The cost model
is `width x height x 4`: an RGBA texture in GPU memory, which is what a WebP
becomes the moment it is uploaded. Every source of every texture is counted, and
for a canvas-backed texture the backing canvas is measured rather than the
declared size — a Phaser `Text` reports its source as 1x1 in this build while
its canvas is the real bitmap.

Identical at 1400x820 and at 844x390. The footprint is viewport-independent: it
is all art plus one world-unit tiling sprite.

| point | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 1. after boot, title live | **304.3 MB**, 184 textures, 5 plates resident | **169.8 MB**, 179 textures, 0 plates |
| 2. on the world map | **365.5 MB**, 223 textures, 5 plates | **231.0 MB**, 218 textures, 0 plates |
| 3. during a level | **308.1 MB**, 188 textures, 5 plates | **205.3 MB**, 184 textures, 1 plate |
| 4. back on the world map after a finished level | **365.6 MB**, 224 textures, 5 plates | **231.0 MB**, 218 textures, 0 plates |

Point 4 is the reported symptom exactly: the level is finished with
`endRun('won')`, the victory dialog appears, and LEVEL SELECT is tapped.

Peak **365.6 MB → 231.0 MB**, a 134.5 MB cut, 36.8%.

### Are plates freed on scene shutdown, or retained?

**Retained, and not by omission in one place — there was no freeing anywhere.**
Before this change, `grep -rn "textures.remove" src/` returned nothing at all.
The only call in the repository was in `tools/harness/index.html`, in a leak
test. Every texture a tab touched lived until the tab died.

The code that does not free them is `src/scenes/GameScene.ts`: it drew the plate
in `drawPlate()` and registered three `shutdown` handlers, none of which
mentioned a texture. The code that loaded them all is
`src/systems/ArtLoader.ts:queueArt`, called once from `BootScene.preload`.

### Total decompressed cost of everything loaded at boot, before any level

**294.5 MB** across the 146 manifest files, plus **9.1 MB** of greyscale copies
built at boot by `ensureGrey` for the 24 keys in `art.greyable`, plus three small
generated textures. Measured live at boot: **304.3 MB over 184 textures**.

By directory, decompressed against on-disk:

| directory | decompressed | on disk | files |
|---|---|---|---|
| maps | 134.47 MB | 5.62 MB | 5 |
| enemies | 50.33 MB | 4.15 MB | 28 |
| ui | 37.82 MB | 1.79 MB | 30 |
| effects | 21.57 MB | 2.39 MB | 15 |
| heroes | 16.31 MB | 1.36 MB | 10 |
| towers | 14.61 MB | 1.29 MB | 14 |
| branding | 6.75 MB | 0.39 MB | 3 |
| abilities | 4.50 MB | 0.55 MB | 18 |
| props | 4.00 MB | 0.55 MB | 5 |
| fx, ui_icons, projectiles, kenney, units | 4.12 MB | 0.27 MB | 18 |

The ratio is the finding in one line: **16x**. On-disk size is not a proxy for
memory and never was.

### Pages artifact size, and how it has grown

The artifact is `dist/`: `public/` copied verbatim (Vite does not hash it, which
is why `stamped()` exists) plus `index.html` and the bundle. `npm install`
answers 403 here so the build cannot be run; `public/` + `index.html` + `src/`
measured out of git is the honest stand-in.

The repository's history is 67 commits and mostly same-day "Add files via
upload", so "the last five deploys" is a shorter window than it sounds. Every
commit on `main` that changed `public/`:

| commit | public/ | maps/ | plates | what |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ce35208` | 27.97 MB | 5.62 MB | 5 | merge of the level 5 branch |
| `0c4c274` | 25.88 MB | 4.88 MB | 4 | transformations, Courtland's third |
| `3e9a32b` | 25.59 MB | 4.88 MB | 4 | story mode pays in cakes |
| `82783b3` | 25.47 MB | 4.88 MB | 4 | hero facing and health |

So the artifact grew **2.50 MB** across that span, of which 0.74 MB is level 5's
plate. Over the same span the decompressed texture cost grew **7.91 MB** — and
would have grown 31.64 MB had level 5's plate matched the others (see step 4).
The artifact size is not the thing that was getting dangerous.

## Step 2 — the three questions

**1. Does GameScene or any scene call `TextureManager.remove` on the plate it
loaded when it shuts down, or does every plate a session touches stay resident
for the life of the tab?**

Neither, before this change, and the distinction matters. No scene called
`remove` on anything. And a plate did not need to be *touched* to be resident:
all five were loaded at boot whether or not the player ever opened a level. A
session that quit from the title screen still paid for all five.

**2. Are the level select cards separate small textures, or is the world map
cropping from the full plates?**

**Separate small textures, and the world map never touches a plate.** Each card
is its own 300x200 WebP under `public/assets/ui/card_levelN.webp`, cut out of the
plate *offline* by `tools/mapcards/run.sh cards` and copied into `public/` by
hand. `WorldMapScene` draws `ART.worldMap.cards[node.level.id]`, which resolves
to `card-levelN`. Five cards cost 1.2 MB decompressed between them.

So entering level select does not force the plates into memory. It did not have
to: they were there from boot. Confirmed after the fix — the world map now
renders all five cards with **zero** plates resident.

There is a stale note in `art.json` under `worldMap._note` saying the cards are
"cropped from each level's own plate", which reads as a runtime crop and is not
one. Left alone; flagged below.

**3. Is anything loaded at boot that is only needed inside a level?**

**Yes: most of it.** Measured rather than reasoned about — the scenario visits
Title, WorldMap and Loadout, collects every texture key those screens actually
draw, and subtracts.

Before the change: **120 of 146 manifest textures, 259.1 MB, never reached the
glass** on any pre-level screen. The largest were the four big plates at 31.6 MB
each, then `map-level5`, `fx-stunned` (7.0 MB), `fx-boss-bolt` (6.1 MB),
`enemy-rooster` (5.9 MB).

One caveat on that number, because the measurement has a blind spot worth
stating: `map-bg` is counted as never-drawn but *is* drawn — through a
`TileSprite`, whose `texture.key` is a generated uuid rather than the source
key. The honest figure is therefore 119 textures / ~253 MB.

After the change the same measurement gives **120 of 146, 124.7 MB** — the plates
are gone from it, and what remains is the enemy, effect and tower art for levels
the player has not started. That is still 124.7 MB of dead weight on the title
screen, and it is the obvious next thing to attack. Not attacked here: the brief
was the plates, and per-family lazy loading is a bigger change than this pass.

## Step 3 — what changed, and why

Everything below follows from the measurements and nothing else.

**`src/systems/Art.ts`** — `PLATE_KEYS` and `isPlateKey()`, derived from
`art.map` with `Object.values`, so a sixth level is a row in `art.json` and no
code. `REQUIRED_SPRITE_KEYS` no longer includes plates: that list answers "did
boot load what it asked for", and boot no longer asks.

**`src/systems/ArtLoader.ts`** — `queueArt` skips plate keys. New `queuePlate`
loads exactly one, and returns `false` if it is already present so a same-level
restart is free.

**`src/scenes/GameScene.ts`** — a two-line `preload()` that queues the plate for
the level about to be played, and a `freePlate()` on shutdown that removes it.

Nothing is drawn during the load, and that is a decision rather than an
omission; see *The load screen that is not there* below.

`freePlate` also removes `gen-arch-near`. That is a fix in its own right and was
found on the way: the arch occluder is **a slice of the plate**, cropped at run
time and cached under one fixed key with `if (!this.textures.exists(key))` — and
never removed. With plates resident forever it was invisible. It would have drawn
level 1's stonework over level 3's road the first time a second level used an
entrance arch, and freeing the plate without freeing the crop would have made
that certain.

**`src/scenes/HudScene.ts`** — `restartRun()` now stops the HUD and lets
GameScene bring it back, instead of restarting both side by side.

That last one is a regression this change caused and is worth spelling out,
because it is the shape of every remaining risk here. GameScene has a `preload`
now, so its `create()` is deferred behind the loader. `restartRun` restarted
GameScene and then restarted itself; the HUD's `create()` therefore ran first,
and the HUD is built out of GameScene's run state — the hand, the hero, the
counters. There was no run yet:

```
TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'def')
    at GameScene.heroDef
    at HudScene.currentSlotDefs
    at HudScene.measureAbilities
    at HudScene.create
```

`GameScene.create()` already launches the HUD itself once it has a run to
describe, and every other path in `src/` — `tryAgain`, `toTitle`, `toWorldMap`,
`goToLevel` — already stops the HUD and lets the board put it back. `restartRun`
was the one that did not.

**`tools/harness/index.html`** — the new `texmem` scenario, and three fixes the
change forced:

- `toTitle()` waited on `map-level1` to decide the loader had finished. Plates
  are not boot art any more, so that wait would have hung **every scenario in
  the file** for its full 60s and then reported that the game did not boot. It
  waits on two boot-time textures now.
- Nine sites started `Game` and `Hud` together. No scene in `src/` does that;
  it worked only because GameScene had nothing to load. They start `Game` and
  let it launch the HUD, which is what the game does.
- `screens`' Game step and `startRun` now wait on the *run* rather than on the
  scene being active, because GameScene is `isActive` while it is still
  preloading.

**Not done, deliberately:** no plate art was shrunk, re-exported or touched.

## The typecheck that tsdiff could not do

Worth writing down, because `tools/tsdiff.sh` warns about exactly this in its own
header and it still got through.

The first commit had a one-line progress bar callback:

```ts
const onProgress = (v: number): void => bar.setSize(240 * v, 8)
```

`setSize` returns the Rectangle, and a concise arrow body declared `: void`
returns it. CI rejected it:

```
src/scenes/GameScene.ts(608,45): error TS2322: Type 'Rectangle' is not assignable to type 'void'.
```

tsdiff reported that commit as introducing one error, and it was the wrong one —
the harmless `Property 'load' does not exist on type 'GameScene'` cascade. It
could not see the real one because without `node_modules` there is no `phaser`,
so `bar` is `any`, and `any` is assignable to `void`. **A new line touching a
Phaser object is unverified locally no matter how clean tsdiff looks.** That is
what the header says and it is true.

The lesson for the next session is not "run tsdiff", which was done. It is that
a clean tsdiff on code that touches a Phaser return value means nothing, and the
cheap defence is to prefer a block body over a concise one on any callback whose
expression is a Phaser call.

## The load screen that is not there

Fixing the return type was one line. Sizing the bar honestly was not, and the
second commit removed the whole thing.

The bar read `this.scale.width`, and `tests/camera.test.ts` rejects that by name
with the message "these read the physical viewport as if it were CSS pixels; use
viewW/viewH". It is right: layout is CSS pixels, the device scale is carried by
a camera zoom, and the physical canvas size is only ever a camera *viewport*.

But `viewW`/`viewH` are wrong here too, for the opposite reason. During
`preload` there is no UI camera yet — `create()` builds it — so the only camera
is the raw one, and its coordinate space is physical pixels. CSS-pixel
coordinates would put the label a third of the way toward the top-left corner on
a dpr-3 phone.

The correct answer is to compose against the 1280x720 design box, and the only
thing that fits a scene to it is `fitCameraToDesign` — which binds a resize
handler through `onSceneResize` that would still be re-fitting `cameras.main`
long after `create()` had handed that camera to `CameraRig`. A rotate mid-run
would then have two things driving the world camera.

So there is no load screen. The gap between tapping a level and the board
appearing is one or two frames of the canvas clear colour, which is `#10161d` —
the same dark chrome as every letterbox in the game, deliberately not the old
grass green precisely so that a bare frame does not read as a fault. The plate
is about a megabyte and comes out of the HTTP cache on every entry after the
first.

**This is the one thing in this pass that is worse than before**, and it is
worth saying plainly rather than burying: a first entry to a level on a slow
connection now shows a dark frame where it used to show the board immediately,
because the board's art used to already be in memory. A proper load screen wants
a shared fitted component that composes in the design box and tears its own
listener down. That is a small piece of work and it is listed below rather than
smuggled in here.

## The next finding, reported and not fixed

**The world map's tiling background is a 6870x2160 canvas: 56.6 MB, the single
largest texture in the game now that the plates are gone.** It is 24.5% of the
231.0 MB the world map now costs.

`WorldMapScene.drawBackground()` makes a `TileSprite` of `roadWidth() +
WORLD_W * 2` by `WORLD_H * 3` — 6870 by 2160 world units. Phaser allocates a
backing canvas at the sprite's full size, so the whole road's length and three
screen-heights of letterbox margin are a real, uploaded texture. The camera can
never show more than 1280x720 of it at once. It is rebuilt every time the world
map is entered and freed when it leaves — measured, the uuid changes and the
count does not climb — so it is not a leak. It is just very large.

The fix is to size the sprite to the camera's view and drive `tilePositionX`
from the scroll, which is what a TileSprite is for. That is a rendering change
to a screen that currently works, so per rule 6 in `CLAUDE.md` it is proposed
here rather than done. It would take the world map from 231.0 MB to roughly
176 MB. **Say the word and it is a short change.**

## Step 4 — level 5's plate is half the resolution of the rest

Reported only. Not upscaled, not changed, not an argument for changing it.

| plate | source | on disk | decompressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `map_level1_v2.webp` | 3840x2160 | 1.08 MB | 31.64 MB |
| `map_level2.webp` | 3840x2160 | 0.98 MB | 31.64 MB |
| `map_level3.webp` | 3840x2160 | 1.23 MB | 31.64 MB |
| `map_level4.webp` | 3840x2160 | 1.59 MB | 31.64 MB |
| **`map_level5.webp`** | **1920x1080** | **0.74 MB** | **7.91 MB** |

Every plate is drawn into the same box — `plate.setDisplaySize(1280, 720)` — so
this is not a different-sized board, it is the same board painted with a quarter
of the pixels. Levels 1–4 supply 3.0 source pixels per world pixel; level 5
supplies 1.5.

Against rule 7 in `CLAUDE.md` — source height >= world height x maxZoom x
devicePixelRatio, which with `maxZoom` 2.37 and dpr capped at 3 wants 720 x 2.37
x 3 = **5119 px** — levels 1–4 supply 2160 (42% of it) and level 5 supplies 1080
(21%). Both are under-provisioned at full zoom on a retina phone; level 5 is
under-provisioned by twice as much, and will read visibly softer next to the
board the player just came from. `RENDER-QUALITY.md` still describes the plate as
1672px, which is stale.

This is an art decision for Cory. The only thing worth adding to it is that the
memory cost of fixing it is now bounded: with one plate resident instead of five,
bringing level 5 up to 3840x2160 costs 23.7 MB at the moment it is played and
nothing at any other time. Before this change it would have cost 23.7 MB from
boot, for the entire session, whether or not anyone played level 5.

## Verification

No browser, no `npm run dev`, no live check. The sandbox cannot reach github.io
and none was attempted. **Whether the crash is gone is Cory's to confirm on his
phone.**

- **Tests** — `node --test 'tests/*.test.ts'`: **989 pass, 0 fail.** (Node 22
  strips types, so the suite runs here without `node_modules`.)
- **Typecheck** — `sh tools/tsdiff.sh 12258d7`: baseline 210 distinct errors,
  working tree 210, **zero introduced**. This was not true of the first commit
  and the difference is the most useful thing in this report; see below.
- **Harness** — `screens` at 667x375, 844x390 and 1400x820, and again at
  844x390 with `INSETS=0,47,21,47`. Every screen matches a control run on the
  unmodified tree object for object: 1-TITLE 30 drawn, 2-WORLDMAP 56,
  3-LOADOUT 104, 4-CUTSCENE 5, 5-GAME 39. The only fault at any size is
  `SMALL Title [title:version-stamp (hidden dev door, not a tap target)]`, which
  the control produces identically. No NOTCH fault with the notch. 375x667 and
  390x844 portrait are correctly gated by the rotate overlay.
- **Pictures read, not just numbers** — `screens-5-game-844x390.png` shows the
  board with its plate, the HUD, the hero and no leftover loading label;
  `screens-2-worldmap-844x390.png` shows all five level cards with zero plates
  resident.
- **Scenarios** — `realboot`, `worldmap`, `maproute` (which covers NEXT LEVEL and
  re-entry), `results`, `difficulty`, `transform`, `bars`, `abilityicons`,
  `powerart`, `music`, `nightfall`, `texmem`. All clean. `maproute` was the
  scenario that caught the HUD race, before the fix above.
- **RESTART** — no scenario covered the pause menu's RESTART, which is the one
  control that frees and reloads a plate in the same frame. `texmem` covers it
  now: the run comes back, the HUD comes back, and exactly one plate is
  resident, at the same 205.3 MB as a fresh level. No leak across a restart.

**What was NOT checked.** Nothing on real hardware or in real Safari; the
harness is headless Chromium with `--disable-gpu`, so these are texture-manager
bytes and not a measurement of what iOS actually allocates — Safari's own
decoded-image copies are on top of this and are not counted. The cold-load cost
of the new per-level plate fetch was not timed on a real network. Levels 2–5
were not played through end to end; the freeing was verified on level 1 and on
the restart path. None of the eight harness scenarios that still drive the
deleted build menu (`ui`, `muzzle`, `buildall`, `rockets`, `retreat`,
`regressions`, `poor`, `typegame`) were run or relied on.

One harness quirk worth knowing rather than trusting: `realboot` formats its
result line as `RESULT *** ... ***` whether or not anything failed, and
`server.py` treats any `RESULT ***` as a fault, so **`realboot` always exits 5**.
The control run on the unmodified tree exits 5 too. Read its log, not its exit
code.
