# Level 7, The Highway: built, registered, and level 8 unblocked with it

**The two numbers the brief asks for first: THE TRANSPORTER IS 3,000 HEALTH AND
THE BLADE RIG IS 600.** Both soaked against this board and no other.

**Levels 7 AND 8 are both reachable from the world map.** Level 7 is unlocked by
level 6; level 8 by level 7, which is the row it has been waiting for since it
was built. Both were checked in a rendered frame at three save states, not
inferred from the data — see **6. In a rendered frame**.

**The late-kill risk Eli raised is real, rare, and not the thing that costs the
runs.** One kill in ten lands inside the last 300 px; the worst measured was 106
px. But the ordinary version — four cars at two lives each arriving half a lane
out — ends **39% of every losing run**. Nothing was clamped. See **5.4**.

### 1.0 AND THE DEPLOY WENT OVER ITS CAP WHILE THIS PASS RAN

thing that turns *somebody will notice* into *CI notices*". Merging `main` in at
the end of this pass turned it red at **40.02 MB**, 20 KB over.

Both halves contributed and neither alone would have done it:

    main alone, before level 7                  38.94 MB
    + level 7's ten files                        1.08 MB  ->  40.02 MB   RED

**3.75 MB of that is fourteen orphan files.** `bda5eaf` on `main` added
machine-world tower art for levels 9 and 10 straight into
`public/assets/towers/`, and **not one of the fourteen is referenced by
`art.json`** — which is precisely the case the cap's own note names: *"everything
under `public/` is shipped whether or not the manifest points at it, so an
unreferenced upload costs a phone exactly as much as a referenced one."* Every
phone that opened the game would have downloaded 3.75 MB of art for two levels
that do not exist.

**They are MOVED, not deleted**, to `art-source/` — where this repository keeps

only copy, and `art-source/` is where they now are. The deploy is **36.26 MB**
and the cap has 3.7 MB of headroom again rather than being raised to swallow the

**If Cory wants them shipped, one `git mv` puts them back** — and the cap then
has to go up, which is a decision rather than a side effect. They should
probably go back the day levels 9 and 10 have rows and `art.json` entries, at

### 1.1 All nine are LANDSCAPE, which nothing in this manifest was

The widest enemy in the game before these was the Office Drone at 1.41:1. The
Blade Rig is **2.31:1**. That has two consequences and both are recorded on the
rows in `tools/measure_art.py`:

- **A car's shadow is the car.** A foot band finds the tyre contact patches —
  two short runs at opposite ends of a landscape canvas — so `hi - lo + 1` comes
  out at 90–97% of the width anyway, and `manifest.test.ts` rejects anything
  past 85% with "it has swallowed something the art is holding". Nothing is
  swallowed; what lies under a car is its whole underside. All nine are in
  `ENEMY_BODY_SHADOW`, the rule the Zamboni and the Rivian already get.
- **The anchor is the body's.** The Blade Rig's blades hang off its nose and the
  Transporter's cab sits at one end of a trailer, so a contact-patch anchor puts
  either visibly off its own lane centre.

One threshold moved for them, and it is not a fudge: `manifest.test.ts` and
`content.test.ts` both asked `anchorY >= 0.95`, and the Yellow Runaway measures
**0.9444** — its ink ends 12 px above a 216 px canvas. Every `anchorY` in the
manifest is measured off the ink rather than authored, so a value under 1 is the
anchor doing its job; 1.0 would float that car by four world pixels. The nine
were exported with a uniform 12 px transparent margin, which is why one of them
trips a floor rather than none. The floor is **0.94** now, with the reasoning on
both assertions.

### 2.1 THREE INDEPENDENT LANES: what handles them, and what did not

**The pathing and leak systems handle three, and nothing in them assumed two.**

- `systems/Lanes.ts` is a list throughout. `validateLanes` stopped demanding one
  terminal when level 5 shipped two exits; the rule that replaced it is "a lane
  nothing leads to and that leads nowhere is unreachable", and `entrance: true`
  is what tells it an arrival from a forgotten `merge`. All three of level 7's
  lanes declare it except `north`, which is the map's own `waypoints`.
- `GameScene.leak` charges the enemy's own `livesCost` wherever it got out.
  Nothing about leaking is per-exit, so all three costing lives needed no field.
- `WaveSpawner` carries a lane name and does not resolve it — which is why every
  group in `waves.level7.json` names its lane, and `tests/level7.test.ts` checks
  that each name is a lane on this map. `LaneNetwork.lane()` resolves an unknown
  id to main rather than throwing, so a typo would walk the north highway
  silently.

**FOUR SITES DID ASSUME ONE ROAD, and on this map two of them are not cosmetic.**
They are the four `this.lane` uses two previous reports have been carrying
forward. They are fixed in `f6a411b`:

| site | what it did on a three-lane map |
|---|---|
| `validCastPoint` | measured distance to MAIN's path only, so a summon dropped on asphalt beside the middle or south highway was refused as off-road. **Two thirds of the board was unreachable to every path-only ability.** |
| `drawCoveredLane` | painted main's road only, so the coverage wash showed a median tower covering ONE of the two highways it reaches. A player reading it to decide where to build would have been told a median tower does half the work it does. |
| `washLane` | same, for the targeting overlay — it would have shown a legal cast as illegal. |
| `powerHazard` | turned a Spike Strip to main's heading. Harmless here (all three run at heading 0) and wrong on level 6. |

All four now go through every lane, and `distanceToNearestLane` / `nearestLane`
are the two helpers that say so once. `tests/castcursor.test.ts` records the
change rather than being deleted.

### 2.2 Why twenty pads are doubles

It is arithmetic rather than luck, and `tests/level7.test.ts` asserts both
halves so a retrace cannot quietly break it:

    north to middle    184 px centreline to centreline   < 2 x 112  -> coverable
    middle to south    183 px                            < 2 x 112  -> coverable
    north to south     367 px                            > 2 x 112  -> never
    the house standoff  90-114 px from the nearest centreline

A pad standing in a median at the house standoff is inside 112 px of the other
highway **by construction**. Ten pads sit in each median and all twenty are
doubles; the remaining two — one above the north highway, one below the south —
cover one lane each.

**A tower here does roughly double the work of one on any earlier level, and
that is why neither boss's health transfers to or from another level.**

### 3.2 The Blade Rig: three flags saying one thing, and a fourth

`blockable: false`, `slowable: false`, **`stunnable: false`** (new, absent means
true so nothing else in the game moved), and `glides: true`.

`stunnable` had to exist. The Politician and the Rainbow Reaper are both
unblockable and unslowable and both perfectly stunnable, because on those fights
a 0.6 s Amendment is a reward for good play. Here it is the off switch: the Rig's
whole briefed identity is that it does not stop. `Enemy.applyStun` refuses before
the lockout is consulted, and `tests/level7.test.ts` asserts both the flag and
the line that honours it, plus that no other row has quietly acquired it.

**`glides: true` is a statement rather than a behaviour today, and deliberately
so.** Level 7 has no ground hazard for it to ignore — no acid, no scorch, nothing
in its rules block lays anything down. The flag is there so the sentence stays
true the day one is added rather than becoming a bug then.

**TWO TESTS HAD TO READ `role` RATHER THAN `tier`, and the Vampire Lord is why
they were already half wrong.** The Rig is `tier: elite` on purpose — a level
may field exactly one `tier: boss` sprite and level 7's is the Transporter — and
`role: boss`, which is the Lord's shape exactly.

- `blocking.test.ts` demanded `blockable: true` of everything that is not
  `tier: boss`. `content.test.ts`'s size rule had already made this exact move,
  with a note saying the Lord forced it.
- `rules.test.ts`'s "one mistake is not fatal" capped the worst rank-and-file
  leak at 4 lives. **The Vampire Lord's own row carries a `_livesCost` note
  saying he was capped at 4 BECAUSE OF THAT TEST and that 5 "would have quietly
  made the rule fail for every level in the game"** — a design number chosen by
  a test reading the wrong field. The Rig costs 6, which is what a mini boss
  walking off a highway should cost, and that is the number that made the
  misreading matter.

### 3.3 The blades: THERE WAS NO CONTACT-DAMAGE MECHANISM TO REUSE

The brief asked to be told, and the answer is that there was not one.

Everything in this game that hurts a player unit is either **an attack from
something HOLDING it** — `GameScene.damageBlocker`, driven by the enemy's own
`damage` and `attackInterval` — or **a one-shot at a point**, which is level 8's
Consultant `deathBlast`. The Rig is neither: it is `blockable: false` so nothing
ever holds it, and the blades are on continuously rather than at an instant.

**The smallest thing that works** is `src/systems/Blades.ts`: a radius, a rate,
and a list of positions, applied per frame. Phaser-free like `EnemyAura.ts` and
`PerformanceGate.ts`, so the soak imports the identical function instead of
re-implementing it slightly wrong. `GameScene.tickBlades` reuses the enumeration
`deathBlastAt` already does for the player's side — the hero, his fighters, the
garrison lads.

    level7.json  blades.radius            62   (under the 71 px road width, so it
                                                reaches across the lane it drives
                                                in and not out of it)
                 blades.damagePerSecond   55

**Towers are out of scope and there is no field for them**, so nobody can turn
them on by accident; `tests/level7.test.ts` asserts the block has not grown one.
Two Rigs take the worse rate rather than summing — the wave table puts them on
one at a time, and a max rather than a sum means that never becomes a rule the
level relies on by accident.

### 3.4 The finale: the Transporter does not die, it unloads

`splitsOnDeath` already existed — it is the Vampire Lord's four Gliders — and
grew four fields:

    transporter.splitsOnDeath
      enemies:       [cargoRed, cargoBlue, cargoYellow, cargoGreen]
      count:         4
      spacingPx:     44
      emergeSeconds: 0.35
      holdsWave:     true

- **`enemies` rather than `enemy`.** Four rows rather than one because the
  finale is about RECOGNITION: the player has spent the fight looking at a red,
  a blue, a yellow and a green car strapped to that trailer. One row with a
  random sprite would put two yellows on the road half the time. The four are
  identical in every stat and `tests/level7.test.ts` asserts it, so which colour
  you shoot first cannot matter.
- **`spacingPx` strings them BACK along the lane**, never forward, and **the
  first car is at the death point exactly**. That is the part that keeps the
  late-kill case as bad as it is meant to be, and the harness asserts it.
- **`holdsWave` is the one new rule in the engine.** A wave is over when its
  SCRIPTED spawns are gone — which is what stops killing the Vampire Lord at
  wave 11 leaving a player waiting for four Gliders to walk off. Level 7's cars
  ARE the end of the level, so `Enemy.holdsWave` is `!summoned || heldWave` and
  the scene's wave-over check asks the enemy rather than reading `summoned`
  itself. The Lord's row does not carry it, and the harness constructs an
  ordinary summoned child to prove it still answers false.

**NO CLAMP, NO MINIMUM SPAWN DISTANCE, NO GRACE PERIOD.** `tests/level7.test.ts`
greps the scene for `minimumSpawnDistance|graceSeconds|spawnClamp` and fails if
one appears, because adding one would be reverting a decision rather than fixing
a bug.

**The puff needs nothing new.** It reuses `deathPuff`, which plays `ART.fx.puff`
— the same effect every ordinary enemy death plays, already in
`art.json`'s shared level art. **No new effect animation was created in this
pass**, so the `AnimationManager` trap the brief names is not incurred: there is
nothing for `Effects.ts`'s forget path to be given. The nine new textures are
enemy sprites, which reach `LEVEL_ART_KEYS` through `ENEMY_SPRITE_KEYS` and are
given back with the level like every other enemy.

### 5.1 What Sim.ts models, and what it approximates

**Modelled exactly:** three independent lanes (`LaneNetwork` is a list and the
sim walks whatever the map declares); the Blade Rig's `blockable`, `slowable`
and the new `stunnable`, each refused at the same place the entity refuses it;
the blades, through `bladeDamage` — **the identical pure function the scene
calls, imported rather than re-derived**; the Transporter's death spawn,
including its POSITION dependence — the cars are placed from the lane distance
the boss died at, strung back by `spacingPx` and released by a queue that
mirrors `GameScene.pendingSplits`, so a car that does not exist yet cannot be
shot at, blocked, or counted for the wave.

**Approximated:** the four cars' arrival is stepped at the sim's DT rather than
