## The three answers the brief asked for first

### 1. Multi-spawn and multi-exit: what the engine could do, and what it cost

**Multiple spawns were already supported.** Levels 3 and 4 have two gates each.
A wave group names a lane, `LaneNetwork.lane()` resolves it, and the enemy
walks in from there. Nothing needed doing.

**Multiple exits were not, and neither was a split.** Three separate things had
to change:

- **`LaneDef.merge` became a list.** A lane could name exactly one
  continuation. `merge` is now `MergeContinuation | MergeContinuation[]`; one
  entry is the merge it always was, several are a split. Levels 3 and 4 need no
  edit at all, down to the JSON. The arm a walker takes is chosen once from a
  number it carries (`routePick`, rolled at spawn) rather than per frame, so an
  enemy standing on a junction cannot flicker between roads, a long frame lands
  on the same arm as ten short ones, and the soak and the scene agree on where
  every enemy went. `pickAt` mixes the junction's own id into that number so a
  route with two splits does not send everything the same way at both.
- **`mainMerge` on `MapDef`.** The trunk is not repeated in `lanes` — it *is*
  `waypoints` — so there was no row to hang a split on.
- **`validateLanes` had to stop rejecting a second exit.** Its rule was
  "exactly one lane may reach the exit", written when every multi-lane map was
  a fork feeding one gate and a second terminal was always a forgotten `merge`.
  What replaces it is narrower and still catches that typo: a lane that reaches
  an exit *and that nothing feeds* is a route with no gate.
- **The gate distances had to become per-lane.** Where an enemy emerges, starts
  to fade and stops existing were one set per *map*, taken from the main lane.
  That was right while only the main lane could reach an exit; with two exits at
  two different distances, everything on the longer arm would have leaked while
  still on the board. `Gateway.laneGates` computes the three per lane and
  `Enemy.gatesHere()` resolves them against the lane being walked, with the
  map-wide set as the fallback — so a single-lane map reads exactly the numbers
  it always did.

**The leak system needed nothing.** `GameScene.leak()` is lane-agnostic: it
charges `livesCost` and counts an escape. Two exits both cost lives for free.

**The soak's pathing model needed nothing either.** It already used the
shipping `LaneNetwork`, `advance()` and `Walker`, and its leak test is
`lane.merge === null && laneDistance >= totalLength`, which is correct for any
number of terminals. Its one change was a *second rng stream* for the route
draw, so adding a per-enemy number could not shift the main stream and move
four levels' published win rates.

**Size:** about 200 lines across `Lanes.ts`, `Gateway.ts`, `types.ts`,
`Enemy.ts` and `GameScene.ts`, plus seven new tests. Verified neutral: level 4
soaked 37/60 with an identical loss distribution before and after.

### 2. …and then the plate turned out to be a different shape

**The brief describes two roads in from the left and two out to the right. The
painting is a four-arm crossroads with three gates and one exit.**

`tools/trace_level5.py` classifies the plate, takes the largest connected run
of road, and reports which rows and columns it touches each edge on:

| edge | road touches at | in world coordinates |
|---|---|---|
| west | y 296–362 | one opening, centre y 329 |
| north | x 457–529 | one opening, centre x 493 |
| south | x 480–581 | one opening, centre x 530 |
| east | y 352–419 | one opening, centre y 385 |

One opening per edge. The right-hand arm was cropped and looked at at full
resolution before this was written, because a second exit would have been the
level's signature: there is a single road east, running between two sections of
castle wall. The three western arms are all on the night side of the seam.

So Level 5 ships as **three gates and one exit**. The split engine work above is
correct, tested and general — it is simply not exercised by this level. It will
be by Level 6, whose two lanes are independent end to end.

### 3. Spike Strip and the Glider — reported, not decided

Cory's second ability is **Spike Strip**: `effect: "hazard"`, 26 damage every
0.6s for 8s, plus a 0.55 slow for 1.2s, `poweredOnly: true`. The Glider is the
rank and file for waves 6 to 12.

As shipped, `level5.json`'s `gliding` block reads the brief literally:
`ignoresGroundSlow: true` **and** `ignoresGroundHazards: true`. Under that,
Spike Strip does nothing at all to the level's main enemy for seven of twelve
waves.

The three options, with what each costs:

| | change | effect |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | `ignoresGroundHazards: false` | The strip still cuts for its full 26 a tick; it just cannot slow. One JSON edit. Keeps "gliding" meaning something. |
| **(b)** | Give Spike Strip a small vertical arc | New ability geometry, new art, and it makes the ability better against everything in the game, not just Gliders. |
| **(c)** | Leave it | Cory is a weak pick on Level 5. Defensible — heroes *should* differ by level — but it is four of five heroes unaffected and one gutted. |

**I have not picked one.** The two flags are separate fields precisely so the
answer is one edit.

One thing that makes this cheaper to decide than it looks: **the soak cannot
see it either way.** `Sim.ts` models only the hero's *first* ability, and Spike
Strip is Cory's second. So whichever way this goes, the 40% below does not move.

## The board

Traced by `tools/trace_level5.py`; re-run with
`python3 tools/trace_level5.py --overlay tools/L5_pads_overlay.png` and **look
at the overlay** as well as the numbers.

- **Road width 62**, the narrowest arm's median, with the junction excluded
  from the measurement — four roads meeting is 150px of paint across and a
  handful of those samples drags a short arm's median right up. The four arms
  are 62 (west), 64 (east), 74 (north) and 90 (south). Wider than level 4's 50
  and level 3's 54.6.
- **Routes: west 1436.4, south 1217.3, north 1170.1** — a 22.7% spread. Levels
  3 and 4 arranged *equal* routes so a split wave arrives together; here that is
  impossible without pushing the north gateway 269px off the plate, which is
  seven seconds of a Thrall walking where nobody can see it. **The arrival times
  are equalised in the wave table instead**, with a per-enemy delay table in
  `waves.level5.json`'s `_lanes`. Every route here is the shortest in the game:
  1436 against level 4's 1479 and level 1's 1977.

### Pads: 14 placed

Matching levels 2, 3 and 4 (15, 15, 14). Verified on the four properties level 3
established:

- **On ground** — each pad's 24px core is on turf the classifier recognises
  (the graveyard's indigo or the meadow's green), clear of crypts, tombstones,
  railings, dead trees, firs, rocks, bushes, pumpkins, walls and banners.
- **In range** — 46 to 110px from the nearest lane centreline, against a tower
  range of 112.
- **Apart** — closest pair 68.15px, over the 68 that `spotRadius` 34 needs.
  Tightest of the five levels; level 4's was 74.11.
- **Both halves reach the middle** — 8 pads on the night ground, 6 on the day,
  and each half was guaranteed 4 within 260px of the junction *before* anything
  competed for the rest.

That last one is not decoration. A single greedy sweep ordered by distance to
the junction puts **all fourteen on the meadow**, because the meadow has the
more open ground beside it, and the crossroads stops being contestable from the
graveyard at all. That is what the first run of the placer actually did.

**Coverage: 85.4% of the whole route is inside some pad's range** — west 81.0%,
east 77.6%, north 100%, south 93.2%. Level 4 covers 45.3% of its tail and is
won or lost before the merge. Level 5 is the other shape: the fight is *at* the
crossroads, and a wave that gets through the middle is very nearly gone.

Two placer bugs were found and fixed, both by looking at the picture:

1. The night field was classified as blocked. The first classifier asked for
   `r >= g` on the night side, which is what a purple field looks like if you
   have not sampled one; the graveyard is `b > g > r`. 25,182 night pixels were
   found against the ~145,000 that are there, and all fourteen pads landed on
   the meadow.
2. The candidate sort ran backwards (`-(jd - clear)` instead of `jd - clear`),
   putting every pad against the four outside edges of the plate — as far from
   the crossroads as the plate allows.

### Facing

All six sprites are drawn facing **right** and are declared `artFacing:
"right"`. Each was viewed at full size before the value was set — nothing in
the engine can derive which way a picture faces, and a wrong value here walked
level 3's boss backwards through the entire map.

### Anchors, and two that needed a rule

`measure_art.py` gained the six enemies. Two could not use the ordinary
foot-band method:

- **The Glider hovers.** The only thing its silhouette puts near the ground is
  the tip of a trailing boot — 50px of a 355px canvas — and anchoring on that
  puts its position under its heel with the body hanging off to the right, 0.22
  of its width off centre. It takes a **body anchor and a body shadow**, the
  rule the Zamboni and the Glitch Bug already have.
- **The Vampire Lord's band is 0.90 and cannot go deeper**, which is the
  opposite of the Thrall's and the same lesson: his weight is on his leading
  boot and his trailing one barely brushes the ground, so at 0.92 only one
  contact survives and the anchor collapses to 0.330 — a fifth of his width off.
  Baby Frank has the mirror problem at 0.94.

Batula was given a foot window as a precaution and did not need one; the window
then excluded both of his actual boot groups and the script raised on an empty
list. Measure first.

## The systems, and how they are scoped

Five new systems, and four levels of tuned numbers that must not move. **A level
names its own rules file on its row in `levels.json`; `Levels.levelRules()`
returns null for a level that names none; every system is a no-op on null.**
Levels 1–4 name none. That is a property of the data rather than a claim about
where an `if` was written, and both the tests and the 480-seed soak hold it.

- **`DayNight.ts`** — the flip is a moment inside wave 5, timed in *seconds from
  that wave's first spawn* and not as a fraction of it. A wave has no length
  until it is over, so a fraction would fire at a different moment in every run
  and the soak could not reproduce it. Speed and sun damage blend across the
  2.5s fade; lifesteal does not, because half a lifesteal is not a thing.
- **`Vampirism.ts`** — stacks that refresh rather than queue, and the
  interaction that makes the level: a bleeding vampire heals for nothing.
- **`AcidPuddle.ts`** — the drop clock runs on **movement**, so a Batula held at
  a standstill is not quietly building a puddle to drop the instant he is free.
  Overlapping puddles take the highest tick, not the sum.
- **`NightRules.ts`** — one nullable object that owns all of the above, held by
  the scene and the soak alike.

### Bleed: which archetypes, and why

There is no `physical` damage type in this codebase; a tower carries an
archetype, and **two of the four cut**:

- **`single-target`** — the Slingshot and the Grinder. Aimed shots that
  puncture one body; the Slingshot's 0.65s interval is what actually builds a
  stack tower.
- **`control`** — the Bramble, which is thorns. Deadfall and Thicket are the
  same weapon.
- **`aoe` is excluded** — the Mortar and the Longshot are explosives, and a
  blast crushes rather than cuts.
- **`support` is excluded** because the Beacon does no damage at all, and so is
  the Ima Dummy for the same reason — bleed applies on hit and a tower that
  deals none never lands one.

The point of drawing the line there rather than everywhere is that it makes
bleed a property of *sustained aimed fire* rather than of splash, which is the
counterplay the level is built on.

**Hero basic attacks and hero abilities both apply bleed** — the brief's
default, and nothing broke under it. It matters more than it looks: the hero is
what a vampire is usually biting, so a hero who could not make one bleed would
be feeding it with no way to stop. The Ima Dummy's lads bleed too, on the same
flag, because they hit like the hero does.

### Bleed damage is flat, and small

`damagePerStackPerSecond: 1.5`, flat, **not** a share of maximum health. Five
stacks on Batula is 7.5 a second against a board that does hundreds. Bleed's
job here is to switch lifesteal off, not to be a damage source — and had it
been a percentage, the sensitivity table below would have come back flat and
told us nothing.

### Batula is the first boss the line can hold

Every boss before him is `blockable: false`, because holding one lets a player
park it and ignore the fight. Batula cannot be parked, because **holding him is
how he wins**: he hits for 55 every 2 seconds and heals for *all* of it, so the
line that stops him is the line feeding him. Made unholdable he would never bite
anything and his lifesteal — the whole point of the level — would never once
come up.

`rules.test.ts`'s "a boss cannot be held" became the rule it was actually
protecting: never holdable *and* harmless.

He is also **slowable**, unlike the Rainbow Reaper. Hers is a clock that runs
whatever she is doing, so a permanent slow turns her fight off. His runs on
*movement*: a slowed Batula covers less ground and drops fewer puddles, which is
a real reward for slowing him rather than an exploit.

## The soak

Simulator: `tools/soak/Sim.ts`, hero **Cory only** (`tools/soak/level.ts` pins
one hero; the seven-hero rotation in `run.ts` was not used, so no Courtland
contamination in any number here). Difficulty **normal**. Seeds 1..N, the same
seeds every run.

### What the simulator models, and what it does not

**Modelled exactly**, sharing the shipping rule modules with the scene: the
day/night phases and their speed and sun-damage modifiers, the dusk conversion
including the health-percentage rule, lifesteal and its cap, bleed and the
bleed-disables-lifesteal interaction, the acid puddles' interval-on-movement,
their damage to the hero and to the lads, their halving of tower fire rate, the
no-stacking rule, and the Humiliation meter's speed and damage bonuses including
the roar's free stack.

**Approximated**: the summoned *fighters* are not on the board in the soak at
all, so a puddle has two kinds of victim here and three in the game.

**Not modelled**: the Spike Strip, and therefore the entire Glider
ground-hazard question. Only the hero's first ability is simulated.
