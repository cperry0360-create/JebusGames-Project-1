## What was actually wrong, twice

Two mechanics were doing nothing and the soak said everything was fine. Both
are written up in full in [`SOAK-REPORT.md`](../SOAK-REPORT.md); the short
version is here because it is the reason every number below is trustworthy and
the previous three were not.

**HAT-GTT's repair had never once run.** `Sim.ts`'s enemy state has no
`maxHealth` field — it reads `def.maxHealth` — so `tickRegens` passed
`undefined`, `NaN` propagated through the guard, and the heal was declined
silently. **A 38% win rate was reported for a boss whose whole mechanic was
inert.** That is the third time this repository has done that (level 4's Beacon
aura, level 8's HR armour aura), so four counters now print **even at zero**,
and which ones print is asked of the LEVEL rather than of the number:

```
regeneration put back 247520 health across the run set (516 a run)
the flame did 52828 damage to the player's own units (110 a run)
towers were switched off for 36264 tower-seconds (75.5 a run)
the blades did 39744 damage to the player's own units (83 a run)
```

**And printing them found the second one.** The blades read exactly 0 — on
level 9 **and on level 7, which was tuned against them**. The only thing they
can cut on any level but the first is the hero (the garrison tower is drafted
on level 1 alone via `extraTowerWeights`, so no soaked board elsewhere has a
soldier standing anywhere — measured, `soldiers 0` over 60 seeds on both
levels), and the sim parks the hero at the main lane's midpoint. A No-Pilot on
the south arm never came within **359 px** of it.

Level 9's half is fixed. **Level 7's is not, and is not mine to fix here**: the
hero's single station is a documented approximation that all eight published
win rates rest on, and moving it would move all eight. It is recorded in
`SOAK-REPORT.md` and it is the first item in "where this leaves the
repository".

## The board

`src/data/map_level9.json` is **generated** by `tools/build_level9_map.py` from
`tools/level9_geometry.json` and is not hand-edited. Every coordinate in it is
the geometry file's; the build script asserts that each lane end it snaps sits
within a quarter of the trace width of the node it is snapping to, and the
worst snap is 6.4 px against a 47.5 px trace.

| | |
|---|---|
| lanes | `north` 1499 px, `south` 586 px, `tail` 1087 px |
| entrances | `north` and `south`, both out of the one painted west mouth |
| exit | `tail`, terminal (1177, 329) — **103 px inside the nearest frame edge** |
| route | 2586 px via north, 1672 via south |
| pads | 15, one centred on each painted grey chip |

**TWO ENTRANCES, ONE EXIT, AND NO FORK IN THE DATA.** The plate paints a trace
that splits just inside the mouth and rejoins at the bottom — a diamond — and
`validateLanes` reports a diamond as "merges in a circle", because its cycle
check shares one visited set across sibling branches instead of keeping one per
path. The runtime underneath is fine; the validator is not. The two arms are
therefore modelled as **two entrance lanes that share the painted mouth and
merge into the tail**, which is levels 3 and 4's shape, needs nothing from the
engine, and puts exactly the same walkers on exactly the same paint. The
validator bug and its one-line fix are in the geometry report; nothing here
depends on it being fixed.

`python3 tools/check_level9.py` re-measures the plate independently (it
area-averages where the tracer point-samples) and agrees:

```
  every painted stretch: re-traced 3283.3, geometry 3284.4, 0.04%
  over the whole route 47.0  geometry 47.5  1.05%  (gate 3%)
  15 chips over 2000 px; the largest neutral-grey blob under the threshold is 224 px
  standoff 69.4-194.3, median 93.3  (levels 2-4 use 90-114)
  PADS THAT CANNOT REACH THE ROUTE at range 132: 1, 7, 15
  PADS COVERING TWO SEPARATE PASSES: 3 of 15 at range 132, 2 at 112
the plate and tools/level9_geometry.json agree.
```

**THREE OF THE FIFTEEN PADS CANNOT REACH THE ROAD** with the shortest attacking
range in `towers.json`, and only three cover two passes of it. This board holds
**less** effective DPS than fifteen pads suggests, not more, and every health
figure above is soaked against it and against no other level. Pad 15 at 194.6 px
is reachable only by the Escalation at 215 — a sniper nest, and a trap for
anyone who builds a Rounding there. That is in the paint; it is reported rather
than moved, because moving a pad means moving it off the chip the artist
painted.

## The four fights, out of systems that already shipped

No new combat engine. One new rule module, `src/systems/Regen.ts`, 40 lines and
Phaser-free, because nothing in the game restored health before.

| | ability | how it is built |
|---|---|---|
| HAT-GTT | WALL OF TEXT | `systems/Flame.ts` — level 6's Rooster breath, re-aimed. Telegraph, corridor, scorch, tower fire-rate penalty, all per-level numbers in `level9.json` |
| HAT-GTT | REGENERATE | `systems/Regen.ts` — **new**. Heals below 75% health, 130 every 11 s, and the sheet plays over the hat while its `-b` tilt is worn for 1.2 s |
| CANCER | claw strike | `systems/TowerDisable.ts` — level 4's Rainbow Reaper clock, with `landFx` added so the impact plays on the tower it just switched off |
| NO-PILOT | unstable charge | `systems/Blades.ts` — level 7's Blade Rig, with `fx`/`fxSize`/`fxCooldown` added so the crash draws at the contact point |
| PERPLEXED | citation storm | `systems/TowerDisable.ts` again, at a different cadence: 1.8 s of dark every 2.6 s instead of 6 s every 7.5 |

All five are Phaser-free modules the soak imports rather than re-implements, so
the simulated fight and the played fight cannot drift.

**The repair corresponds to actual health restoration.** `tickRegen` returns the
number healed, the scene calls `e.heal(r.healed)` with it, and the soak's
`healedTotal` is the sum of the same returns — 516 health a run, measured. **The
effect plays OVER the hat**; no second hat is drawn from the effect sheet. The
alternate sprite is `enemy-hat-gtt-b`, the same hat at a different tilt, worn
for the length of the repair and put back.

**PERPLEXED's storm is a short blackout, not a new status system.** The brief
asked for something the engine already communicates cleanly and explicitly said
not to invent a status for a joke; `towerDisable` is the existing one, it
already has a stunned overlay and a windup telegraph, and the only change was
letting a carrier name its own landing effect.

**CANCER is not a medical reference.** No medical symbols, no zodiac text: it is
a claw out of a constellation, and the name is the constellation. The crab faces
FORWARD in its source art rather than along its travel — **noted, not fixed**, as
the manifest asks. Frames 4-6 of `fx_crab_claw_impact` clip debris at the cell
edge; **cosmetic, not re-exported**, as the manifest asks.

### Facing

Taken from the manifest, not re-derived. Three sprites carry `artFacing: 'right'`

## The fourth regular, and the one that hovers

`lvl9-miniboss-server.png` is, **despite its filename, the fourth REGULAR
enemy**. The source PNG keeps its name; the manifest key is `enemy-server-walker`
and the enemy is `serverWalker` — an armoured 500-health wall, `role: armored`,
`tier: elite`, costing 2 lives. The four regulars are the packet (fast, 72), the
corrupt (basic, 175), the data bug (140, hovers) and the server walker.

**The data bug carries `glides: true` and is NOT an air unit.** There is no
`layer` field and there must not be: every tower in the game can shoot it, and
an air layer would make it invisible to a ground-only gun on a board with no
anti-air tier to build around it. **What `glides` actually does on this board is
nothing yet**, and that is worth saying plainly: both its effects — ground
hazards miss, ground slow misses — are read off `NightRules.gliding`, and
`NightRules.from` returns null for any level without a `phases` block. Level 9
has no day/night clock and is not growing one to hold a hover. So the flag is a
STATEMENT here, exactly as level 7's Blade Rig says of its own copy, and the
hover is in the picture. The harness checks the half that would break the level:
it is not on an air layer, and a ground tower can still hurt it (140 → 118 in
1.6 s).

**Every display name is in one place.** `enemies.json`'s `name` and `flavor` on
the eight rows are what the HUD, the boss bar and every log line print, and
`level9.json`'s `roster` maps the slot to the id. Renaming them with the kids is
eight edits in one file.

## The prop layer, and Vlaude

**The manifest is right that there was no prop layer.** There was one hard-coded
prop in the game — level 1's tavern sign, built by `GameScene.buildSign` — and
nothing general.

**What was done:** `MapDef.scenery` and `GameScene.buildScenery`, which is
`buildSign` generalised. Each entry is a manifest key, a world position and a
size; `loop: true` marks an animation. It adds an image or a sprite, y-sorts it
and never looks at it again. **Nothing in it has health, takes damage, blocks a
shot, occupies a spot or appears in any combat list** — there is no field to put
those in, which is the point.

**Vlaude is scenery.** He is on a damaged screen at (1150, 630), bottom right,
in the data bloom beside the door he is behind. Measured from the live scene:
**302 px off the nearest centreline, 203 px from the nearest pad**, drawn
116x124. He is not in `enemies`, not in `towers`, has no targeting and no
behaviour. The harness asserts all of it, and a mutation that moves him onto the
board fails the check.

**The three electrical arcs are a seamless loop and nothing else.** `repeat: -1`
is the only looping play in the game; every other effect is a one-shot that
destroys itself on `ANIMATION_COMPLETE`. They are placed on the machinery in the
corners: the closest any of them comes to a centreline is 128 px and to a pad
132 px, so none covers a road, sits on a build spot or stands between a tower
and a target. They are not an attack, a hazard or a projectile.

## The ending

`level9.json`'s `rupture` block, played once, on a win only, centred on the exit
terminal the map already declares — because the fiction is that the way out
opens where the enemies were going.

```json
"rupture": { "fx": "fx-tile-shatter", "x": 1177, "y": 329,
             "size": 420, "durationMs": 1400, "delayMs": 500, "shakeMs": 700 }
```

Above the map (`LAYER.worldOverlay + 20`) and below the results dialog, which is
on the fixed UI camera. The dialog is held for `delayMs + durationMs` so the
level's ending is not staged behind its own scoreboard.

**It was playing where nobody was looking, and only a rendered frame could have
said so.** The world camera shows about a third of the board at a phone's zoom
and the door is off the right of it unless the player had panned there
themselves. Every number about the sprite was correct: it existed, it was at
(1177, 329), it was on frame 7 of 8, it cleaned itself up. `CameraRig.lookAt`
now points the rig at the rupture on the frame the run ends, and `delayMs` is
the time the camera has to arrive rather than a pause for its own sake.

**There is no white-flash final-defeat animation in this repository.** It was not
looked for.

### And then the comic

The brief asks the level to advance through the existing cutscene system toward
level 10 with `comic_l9_end_gate_A.png`. **The existing system could not do
that**: `cutscenes.json`'s `levels` map means "what plays BEFORE this level", and
every reader of it — `CutsceneScene.init`, `shouldPlay`, the loadout hand-over —
means exactly that. That panel is neither level 9's opening nor anything level 10
can carry while level 10 has no row in `levels.json`.

**What was added is one map and one argument.** `outros` in `cutscenes.json`,
held to the same rules by the same `cutsceneProblems` (a real level, a non-empty
list, panels under `cutscenes/`, no repeats), and `CutsceneRequest.panels` so the
scene is told which panels rather than looking them up a second way. The way off
a **won** level goes through it: `GameScene.leaveWon(then, prepare?)` plays the
outro if the level has one and starts `then` either way, so it is a no-op on
every other level by construction. A loss does not play it — the door opens
because the gauntlet was beaten.

The panel is `art-source/comic_l9_end_gate_A.png` at WebP q95, 1672x941,
0.58 MB, PSNR 33.8 dB. It ends on the LEVEL 10 FINAL PROTOCOL gate.
`comic_l9_no_pilot.png` and `comic_l9_perplexed.png` are in `art-source/` and
are **not** converted or wired: the brief named two comics and those are the
two. Level 10's assets are not mine and none were touched.

## The balance

Every number is in JSON under `src/data/`. Nothing that affects gameplay is
hardcoded in a `.ts` file.

The full sensitivity tables, the loss distribution, and the reasoning for the
two flat tables are in [`SOAK-REPORT.md`](../SOAK-REPORT.md). The summary:

- **HAT-GTT is held up by its REPAIR, not by health or armour.** It opened at
  1980 health with a 420 repair and escaped 40 runs in 40. Armour moved the win
  rate by one point; health by three; the repair by nine.
- **CANCER is held up by its CLAW.** `cooldown` takes the level from 36% to 44%
  across its range; its armour table is flat.
- **NO-PILOT is held up by SPEED**, 43% to 28% across 34-82 px/s. Its blades are
  worth 83 damage a run — real and small.
- **PERPLEXED sets the level.** Health moves it 52% to 29%; the storm's
  `duration` moves it 55% to **4%**.

**Two armour tables came back flat and the reason is structural.** The armour is
not doing nothing — escapes climb monotonically with it (HAT-GTT 38 → 41 → 47,
CANCER 21 → 28 → 40). What is flat is the WIN RATE, because more armour early
means more runs die early, which means fewer runs reach PERPLEXED, which means
fewer deaths there. The losses move rather than multiply. So the early mini
bosses' armour is chosen on feel and the win rate is set by PERPLEXED's dials.

**One wave change.** NO-PILOT moved from the south arm to the north: south is
1672 px against north's 2586, and at 58 px/s it reached the door in 29 seconds
past a third of the guns and walked out of 16 of 40 runs. Every boss comes down
the long arm now and the escorts alternate. It is also the lane on which its
blades could ever touch anything — both reasons are recorded on the wave.

**Levels 1 to 8 are byte-identical.** Same 480 seeds, this tree against
`origin/main`, `diff` reports nothing:

```
level1 89%  level2 53%  level3 88%  level4 62%
level5 45%  level6 44%  level7 41%  level8 41%
```

### The seven effect sheets, and the global animation manager

`Phaser.AnimationManager` is global, so an animation left cut against a texture a
level has freed plays zero frames and draws nothing, and `anims.exists` then
skips re-cutting it against the sheet the next level just loaded. Every new
effect here goes through `registerEffectAnims`/`forgetEffectAnims` automatically,
because both walk `ART.files` for a `render[key].sheet` entry and all seven have
one. Verified rather than assumed:

```
  level 9: 82 keys asked for, 82 resident
    17 of 17 effect animations cut against live textures
  level 1 again: 51 keys asked for, 51 resident
    10 of 17 effect animations cut against live textures
  back on the world map: 0 of 133 level-art keys still resident
```

`levelart` now walks level 9 too, and its sheet check had to be **scoped** to get
there: it asserted every animated sheet in the manifest on every level, which was
true while every sheet was boot art and reported seven faults a level the moment
level 9's became level art. The other half of the claim is now checked
explicitly — a sheet a level does **not** fetch must not be left cut against a
freed texture — and nothing is.

### Three bugs the frames found

1. **The scenery doubled on a restart.** `sceneryArt` and `archOccluders` are
   field initialisers, which run once, and both `create()` paths append — so a
   second run on level 9 held four live objects and four the restart had already
   destroyed. **The arch half has been there unnoticed since the occluders
   landed**; nothing counted them across a restart.
2. **The ending played off screen** (above).
3. **The closing comic had nowhere to live** (above).

### The card crop

`public/assets/ui/card_level9.webp`, cropped from `map_level9.webp` by
`tools/mapcards`: **`[0.66, 0.2565, 0.34, 0.403]`** — x from 66% of the plate

the trace coming down from the top left. A crop of the big loop on the west half
would be a cyan squiggle on a blue board, which is the card level 1 nearly got
and the card level 5 was rejected for.

### The 7x rule

`source height >= world height x maxZoom x devicePixelRatio`, which at today's
maxZoom 2.37 and a dpr capped at 3 is **7.11x**.

**The nine cast sprites meet it**, which is what matters most — they are the
things with hard outlines that a heavy minification turns to grey smear:

| key | world px | source px | needs | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `enemy-packet` | 56 | 404 | 398 | 1.01x |
| `enemy-corrupt` | 66 | 537 | 469 | 1.14x |
| `enemy-data-bug` | 70 | 607 | 498 | 1.22x |
| `enemy-server-walker` | 85 | 697 | 604 | 1.15x |
| `enemy-hat-gtt` | 145 | 1031 | 1031 | 1.00x |
| `enemy-hat-gtt-b` | 145 | 1028 | 1031 | **0.997x** |
| `enemy-cancer` | 100 | 718 | 711 | 1.01x |
| `enemy-nopilot` | 140 | 996 | 995 | 1.00x |
| `enemy-perplexed` | 132 | 981 | 939 | 1.05x |

`enemy-hat-gtt-b` is **3 pixels short**, 0.3%. It is deliberate: the two hat
sprites must draw at the same height or the swap during a repair would visibly
jump, so both are at 145 and the `-b` source happens to be 3 px shorter than the
`-a`. Raising it to 1031 would mean the two hats are different sizes, which is a
worse fault than 0.3%.

**All seven effect sheets fall short**, and this is the honest answer to the
brief's question:

| key | drawn at | source | needs | ratio | where the size comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fx-wall-of-text` | 230 | 512 | 1635 | 0.31x | scaled to `flame.reach` |
| `fx-hat-regenerate` | 190 | 512 | 1351 | 0.38x | `hatGtt.regen.fxSize` |
| `fx-crab-claw-impact` | 190 | 512 | 1351 | 0.38x | `cancer.towerDisable.landFxSize` |
| `fx-nopilot-crash` | 165 | 512 | 1173 | 0.44x | `blades.fxSize` |
| `fx-perplexed-query` | 165 | 322 | 1173 | 0.27x | `perplexed.towerDisable.landFxSize` |
| `fx-tile-shatter` | 420 | 724 | 2986 | 0.24x | `rupture.size` |
| `fx-electrical-arc` | 105 | 512 | 747 | 0.69x | `map_level9.json` scenery |

These are **magnified** on a retina phone, by up to 4x, which the rule says is a
soft blurry sprite. It is much less objectionable on an effect than on a
character — they are in motion, they are on screen for under a second, and most
of their content is glow rather than outline — but it is short and the fix is a
re-export at a bigger cell. None of them can be fixed by re-encoding; the sources
are the size they are. **Recorded rather than worked around.**

### The budget

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| files under `public/assets/` | 242 | 267 |
| whole deploy | 40.39 MB | **47.85 MB** |
| boot path | 6.55 MB | **6.57 MB** |
| worst single level | 9.74 MB (level 8) | **16.96 MB (level 9)** |
| music (streams) | 6.41 MB | 6.41 MB |

**The boot path moved by 0.02 MB**, which is the number a player actually waits
on, and the 0.02 is the world-map card — boot art by design, because the map
screen draws every level's card. Everything else level 9 brought is level art
and `queueArt` skips level art at boot.

**It nearly moved by 0.21, and finding out why is the most useful thing in this
section.** `enemy-hat-gtt-b` — the hat's alternate tilt, worn while it repairs
itself — is named by `regen.altSprite` and **not** by `sprite`, so
`ENEMY_SPRITE_KEYS` did not derive it, nothing classified it as level art, and
0.19 MB of level 9's cast sat on the loading bar for every player on every
level, freed by nothing. **No test caught it**: `levelart.test.ts` checks that
nothing in a level's list is missing, not that nothing missing from a list ought
to be in it. It was found by diffing the boot path against `main`, file by file.
It is in `art.levelArt.byLevel.level9` by hand now and the reason is written
there, because the next field that names a second sprite for an enemy will need
the same row.

What moved is what a player downloads when they open level 9, and **level 9 is
the heaviest level in the game by a wide margin** — 16.96 MB against level 8's
9.74. **3.75 MB of the difference is the machine tower skin**, which level 9 is
the first board to wear, and 1.16 is its two comics.

`tests/content.test.ts`'s single deploy cap was **split into four** on the
commit before this one, and the split is the point: boot, one level, music and
the whole deploy are four different costs and one number could not see the
difference. Its per-level measurement counted only the pre-level comic, which
under-read level 9 by the 0.58 MB of the closing panel — it counts `outros` too
now, and the cap is 18 MB against 16.96.

**The obvious saving was tried, measured and rejected.** Three reports have now
said the fourteen skin files are 2.4-3.6x the bytes of the identically sized
originals they repaint and that re-encoding would give back about 2.4 MB. They
were re-encoded through `tools/towebp` at the house q95: **PSNR 28.5 to 36.4 dB,
six of the fourteen under 32** — a second lossy pass on already-lossy art, and
visibly so on flat colour. The originals were restored from a scratch backup. The
2.4 MB is real and it is not free; the honest version is a re-export from
whatever the artist drew them in, and those sources are not in this repository.

**Level 10 will not fit.** It wears the same skin, needs the same shared art and
brings a 3840x2160 plate of its own, so it lands within a few hundred KB of level
9 before a single enemy is converted. That is the point at which somebody has to
choose between the re-export and a thinner shared list.

## Typechecking

```
sh tools/tsdiff.sh 4818e52
baseline 4818e52: 213 distinct errors; working tree: 213

```

**Zero introduced, and no new-file warning**, which means no file added by this
work imports Phaser — so `tsdiff`'s one blind spot (a new file where `Phaser` is
`any` and every member access typechecks locally no matter what it says) is not
in play here. `src/systems/Regen.ts` is Phaser-free on purpose, like every other
systems module.

One naming decision is a finding rather than taste: the scenery field on
GameScene is `sceneryArt` and **not** `scenery`, because with a field of that
name every `this.scene` in that file — already an unresolved Phaser member
without `node_modules` — turns from TS2339 into TS2551 "did you mean 'scenery'?",
and `tsdiff` reports the lot as newly introduced. Same errors, different code,
and about forty minutes to work out.

## Where this leaves the repository

**IN FLIGHT — nothing.** Level 9 is built, registered, soaked inside the band,
verified from frames at three viewports, and green on CI.

**OPEN, carried forward:**

1. **Level 7's blades do nothing in the soak, and level 7 was tuned against
   that.** Measured here as exactly 0 damage a run over 60 seeds. The cause is
   the sim's single parked hero plus the garrison tower being a level-1-only
   draft. Fixing it means moving the hero model, which moves all eight published
   win rates, so it needs its own pass and its own re-tune. **This is the biggest
   open item in this report.**
2. **`validateLanes` rejects a diamond** — one visited set shared across sibling
   branches instead of one per path. Level 9 does not need it fixed (it models
   the fork as two entrance lanes), but the next branching map might. The
   one-line fix is in the geometry report.
3. **The seven effect sheets are under the 7x rule**, by 0.24x to 0.69x. A
   re-export at a bigger cell is the fix; nothing in the repository can do it.
4. **Level 10 does not fit the per-level budget** and the machine skin cannot be
   re-encoded to make room. Somebody has to choose between an artist re-export
   and a thinner shared art list, and the cap in `tests/content.test.ts` is where
   it will come up.
5. **Three of level 9's fifteen pads cannot reach the road** at the shortest
   tower range, and pad 15 is Escalation-only. That is in the paint and is a
   design question, not a bug — but it is a trap for a player who builds the
   wrong tower there and nothing on screen says so.
6. **`comic_l9_no_pilot.png` and `comic_l9_perplexed.png`** are in `art-source/`
   and not wired. They are mid-level comics for a system that only plays comics
   at the start and (now) the end of a level, and nothing asked for them.
7. **The `screens` version-stamp SMALL fault** is pre-existing at both phone
   sizes and is a hidden dev door the harness itself annotates as not a tap
   target. Either the harness should stop counting it or the stamp should be
   44 pt.

**WAITING ON A DECISION — the working names.** Every display name is in
`enemies.json` and nowhere else, so HAT-GTT, CANCER, NO-PILOT, PERPLEXED, Packet,
Corrupt Byte, Data Bug and Server Walker are eight edits in one file whenever
Cory and the kids want to rename them.
